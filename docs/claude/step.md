## STEP files (decided in phase 7)

The design is document 55 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 7.A.

- **Crate.** `ryustep` sits beside the kernel: ryukei never depends on it, and it holds no C++ (OCCT and SMLib stay references in `ryukei-bench`). Healing lives in `ryukei::heal`, for every importer.
- **Part 21.** `part21::parse` reads a file into a `File`: the header's entities as written, then every data section's instances (`Record::Simple` or `Complex`, each a `Part` of a name and `Param`s) in one table sorted by instance number. Statements are found in one pass (a `;` outside strings and comments) and parsed in parallel; the first error in the file wins on any number of threads. Liberties real files need: keywords and enumerations in any case (held upper case), line breaks inside strings dropped (writers wrap them), a backslash that starts no directive kept, bytes above 127 UTF-8 if they are else ISO 8859-1, `\S\` on pages other than A as U+FFFD, integers where reals go (exactly, up to 2^53), anchor and reference sections skipped, anything after `END-ISO-10303-21;` ignored. Faults are `StepError` (`NotPart21`, `Syntax` by line and byte column, `DuplicateInstance`, `UnknownSchema`, `NoData`); lists nest at most 64 deep.
- **Writing.** `part21::write` is the inverse: one statement a line, instances in table order, reals as the shortest text that reads back to the same bits (always a point, an exponent outside `[1e-5, 1e16)`), every non-printable run of a string as one `\X2\` (or `\X4\`) run. Anything Part 21 can't hold is `WriteError::Unwritable`. Any table read writes and reads back bit for bit.
- **Schema.** `schema::Schema::of` takes the first known schema in `FILE_SCHEMA` (AP203 both editions, AP214, AP242), compared up to the first character that can't be in an identifier (OCCT puts a point after AP242's). `schema::Entities::decode` types every instance in parallel: entities it doesn't read are `Entity::Other` (a complex instance's names joined by spaces), and an instance that breaks its entity's rules holds its `ImportError`, failing only what needs it. Values stay as written (the file's units, references by instance number). It reads points, directions, vectors, placements; lines, conics, B-splines of every knot form (rational ones from complex instances, by part name, with or without the attribute-less parts), trimmed, composite, offset and polyline curves, pcurves and surface, seam and intersection curves; elementary, B-spline, swept, offset and rectangular trimmed surfaces; vertices to shells and the four B-rep items; representations, mapped items, relationships, transformations, contexts, units and measures; products to assembly usages.
- **Units.** `Entities::units` gives a context's length unit in meters, its plane angle unit in radians (if any) and its `distance_accuracy_value` in meters. SI prefixes are exact powers of ten; a conversion-based unit known by name (inch, foot, yard, mile, mil, degree) within a millionth of its size is taken as exactly it. No length unit is `MissingUnit`: none is guessed.
- **Products.** `products::Products::build` gives every product definition with its shapes (the representation its shape definition names, and those related to it without a transformation, holding B-rep or mapped items), its usages placed by their relationship's transformation (`Transform::Items { child, parent }` or an operator), oriented child to parent whichever way the file related them, the roots, and representations with B-rep items nothing reaches. Faults in one definition, usage or item are collected in `faults`; a usage closing a cycle is dropped as `ReferenceCycle`.
- **Testing.** Unit tests per token, directive, entity and rule; property tests (any text encodes and decodes to itself; random tables write and read back bit for bit); the OCCT files read, write back, decode with only the expected entities left as `Other`, give their units and product trees; one thread and many parse and decode alike; the `step` fuzz target (raw bytes: parse, write back, decode, the product tree and every context's units, never a panic), seeded from the OCCT files.

## Bodies both ways (decided in phase 7)

The design is document 55 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 7.B.

- **Shapes.** `ryukei::heal::Shape` is a solid as a file describes it: geometry in arrays, vertices (`seam` marks one a writer added only to end a seam), edges with intervals, faces with loops of uses (each with the file's pcurve, if any) or a lone vertex, shells and solids with their voids; tolerances are the bounds of approximations, or 0. `heal::heal(key, &shape, &HealOptions)` returns a body, its journal and a `HealReport`; refusals are `Error::InvalidHeal(HealError)`: `Malformed`, `OpenShell` (every free edge), `NonManifold`, `Misoriented`, `BadSeam`, `BadDomain`, `NoPcurve`, `GapTooLarge`, `Uncertain`. Nothing is built until every step has succeeded.
- **Names.** Every vertex, edge and face of the shape is named before anything changes: SipHash of the caller's key, `name::ImportRole` and a hash of its geometry as read (the dump's bits): a vertex its point; an edge its curve, interval and end points sorted; a face its surface, sense and its edges' hashes sorted, but not its seams. Equal hashes take ordinals ranked by neighbours' hashes. A file has no identity of its own to offer, so a face whose boundary changed, or that moved, is a new face.
- **Healing (7.B's steps).** Every edge used twice, once each way. Seams (an edge one face uses twice, along a parameter line of a periodic direction) come out: one loop splits, two join; a piece left empty keeps its vertex only at an apex (the normal turns round it) and not if a writer marked it; a file's vertex loop at a pole goes, elsewhere stays; a marked vertex left between two edges on one curve goes and they are joined (a closed edge of one period, or a clamped closed curve's whole domain), named from every file edge they join. Every use gets a pcurve: exact, else the file's with its affine map found (identity, ends, or a turn either way) and checked at 17 samples, else fitted (512 samples at most; an edge more than ten tolerances off the surface is `GapTooLarge` at once), the edge's tolerance covering the fit's bound. The shape is laid out in the order of its import names and built through `layout` (the boolean's shell builder, now shared), each solid's voids in its region; geometry is renumbered by first use, tolerances cover gaps at curves' ends and approximations up to `tolerance_cap` (1e-4 m), and `check::check` must hold.
- **Seams on export.** `heal::seamed(&body, pipe_tolerance)` gives a body as a file must describe it. A face wraps where a loop winds round its surface or every loop is a hole; its seam is the parameter line at a vertex's projected parameter (vertices ordered by their points' bits), else the surface's own seam, a middle between those, or a fixed grid over the period, that crosses each winding loop once and keeps clear of the others; it runs from the loop below to the one above, or to a pole, where a cone's apex or a new marked vertex ends it; a torus face with no winding loop gets two seams through one vertex. Loops are read by projecting their edges' points, never through pcurves; a loop crossed inside an edge is split there, at the crossing bisected to neighbouring floats; a split closed periodic edge ends one period after it starts. Seam curves run on the surface's own parameter (a cone's ruling as a degree 1 spline; `NurbsSurface::iso` for splines), with iso-line pcurves. Pipes become NURBS within the tolerance, periodic round a closed spine. Faces are cut in the order of their geometry's names and the shape comes out in that order, so a body and the same body read back are written alike. A loop through a pole winds round neither free direction (the jump across a pole is free, as the mesher reads it), so a sphere face bounded through its poles needs no seam. A face on a torus whose loop winds round it both ways is `BadDomain`.
- **Reading.** `ryustep::read(bytes, &ReadOptions)` reads every representation holding B-rep items as one body (a solid region per item), in parallel, in instance order, each `Imported` with its representation, items, product definition and result. Geometry is ryukei's in meters and radians: frames and directions kept bit for bit where already unit to 1e-12 (`Frame::from_axes`, `UnitVector3::exact`, `Line::from_unit`, `Extrusion::from_unit`); conics and elementary surfaces (an ellipse turned where its first axis is the shorter, a cone moved off an apex placement); B-splines clamped as written, unclamped ones periodic where their control points wrap and else clamped by knot insertion, a surface direction clamped with ends meeting made periodic, points kept as written; extrusions; revolutions on the file's axis with x towards the curve; polylines as degree 1 splines; trims, curves on surfaces and rectangular trimmed surfaces as their bases; offsets of analytic surfaces, lines and circles exact, others `NotBounded` (spline offsets come with 7.C); parabolas and hyperbolas as exact arcs between their edge's vertices. Edges take intervals from their vertices; oriented shells and faces wrapping each other past 64 deep are `ReferenceCycle`. `Opened::body_with(k, &ReadOptions, &Work)` heals under a work budget and cancel; a stop is `ImportError::Work(ryukei::Error)` (plan 15.A), and `examples/heal-units` prints each body's units.
- **Writing.** `ryustep::write(&WriteModel, &WriteOptions)` writes products (bodies and placed children) as AP242 or AP214, in meters or millimetres (one division), the uncertainty the largest tolerance. A product's one body goes in its own representation, several each in a related one. Solids are manifold solid breps or breps with voids (voids written outward and used against their orientation), advanced faces, oriented edges, edge curves running with their curves as surface or seam curves where pcurves keep their kind under the surface's units; NURBS as B-splines with knots (rational as complex instances, periodic unclamped from their first knot's last copy, their first `degree + 1 - m` control points again, `m` that knot's multiplicity, as OCCT writes them: given `degree` again whatever `m`, OCCT dropped too few and read a NURBS cylinder as its control square; plan 8.F); extrusions and revolutions as swept surfaces; seam ends named `ryukei:seam`. Instances are numbered by a fixed walk and the time stamp is the caller's. `WriteError::NotRepresentable` names a face that can't be written.
- **Round trips.** A body written and read back has its topology and the bits of its points, curves and surfaces (a revolution's x taken from its curve, a pipe as a NURBS; intervals within resolution, tolerances measured, import names); from the first file written from a body read back, files and bodies go round bit for bit. Millimetre files come back within a few ulps, and go round to a fixed point from there: a pcurve's lengths are written divided by the unit as 3D points are (multiplied by its inverse, a fitted pcurve's control points moved an ulp each time round, #495).
- **Testing.** Unit tests per step and kind, `tests/heal_props.rs` (ryukei: seeded bodies through `seamed` and `heal`) and `tests/round_trip_props.rs` (ryustep: through STEP), release only; `tests/read.rs` (every OCCT file to its volume) and `tests/write.rs`; golden hashes (ryukei's `heal` section, ryustep's `tests/golden/step.txt`); the `step` fuzz target reads bodies (an `ImportError` or a body that checks). Limits: 2 of 1,402 seeded booleans make a torus face that spirals (`NotRepresentable`); fuzzed splines can take tens of seconds in projection and fitting.

## Healing (decided in phase 7)

The design is document 55 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 7.C.

- **Order.** `heal::heal` names the shape, sews, checks every edge is used twice, takes out seams and poles, orients loops and faces, tightens, measures gaps, collapses (and measures again), finds pcurves, builds every connected piece as a solid of its own, judges the shells (building again if they need it), and checks. Nothing is built until every step before has succeeded.
- **Sewing.** Free edges (used once) pair when both ends and 17 samples along each lie within the cap of the other and that gap is under half the shorter one's chord, or (an edge too short for that, #374) the pairs taken have already joined both ends of two free edges of different faces and of no third; candidates from boxes swept along x, nearest pairs first, ties by name. Two edges of one face never pair (files share their seams), nor a closed edge and an open one; a free edge with two candidates coinciding with it is `NonManifold`. A pair becomes an edge on the least-named one's curve, `Merged` from both; vertices joined by the pairs' ends become one at the least-named member's point, `Merged` from all.
- **Seams and apexes.** A seam is an edge one face uses twice whose points lie within the cap of a parameter line of a periodic direction; a lone vertex within the cap of a collapsed row is an apex (kept) or a pole (dropped) by whether the normal turns there.
- **Orientation.** Loops are read by projecting their edges' points (never pcurves): where they alone fix the region (a surface that doesn't close up, loops winding round no period), each must run counterclockwise about the normal at even depth and clockwise at odd, else it is reversed (crossing loops are `SelfCrossingLoop`). On a surface periodic one way that doesn't close up, two loops each once round the period also fix the region, the band between them: run against the face's sense (KiCad's horn tori, whose bands are written with their neighbours' edge uses, #364) both are reversed, where turning the face over by its neighbours kept the band's complement. On a surface that closes up (a torus, a revolution with poles at both ends) both regions are faces, so a face there turned against most of its connected piece, whose region folds back over its neighbours at every edge where their surfaces are tangent (at least one), keeps the file's sense and has its loops alone reversed: KiCad's ring tori are written so too, and turning them over kept three quarters of a fillet's tube, which crossed the solid. A face turned whole by a writer (sense and loops, the fault `heal_props` injects) continues its neighbours and is turned back as before. Each use is read at 16 samples, a step halved where it turns a periodic parameter by a quarter period or more, by its ends or by the parameter velocity at either (a coil's helix of nine turns was read as stepping backwards, its loop reversed and its face left with no region: #473); every other loop reads as before. A lone loop whose area rounding can't sign (two edges along one line, a sliver a file wrote as a face) is left as the file has it, for collapsing to take out (#449). Faces are turned over from each connected piece's least-named face until every edge is used once each way (`Misoriented` for a Möbius band).
- **Shells** (`heal::shells`). The first build makes every shell a solid; each shell's volume sign says which way it faces: summed exactly over fans (`predicates::volume_sign`) for planar faces with straight edges, else from a mesh (chord a hundredth of the body's size) within `4 × chord × curved area`, or where that can't sign it (v0.8.2, #239) from the shell's winding number about a point stepped off a triangle along its normal (two, four or eight bands, a band the chord and the shell's tolerances) further than a band from the mesh: the mesh is closed by index and within a band of the shell, so their winding numbers there agree, decided by a ray's signed crossings with exact orientation tests, +1 where the faces point out; each mesh an eighth as coarse as the last (it jumped to what the measured volume needed, down to a sixty-fourth: the Voron 0.2's 0.9 mm filament, 0.3 m long, went from 3 mm at once to 48 µm, 248,000 spline triangles, where 380 µm and 79,000 let its winding decide), five meshes at most and 4M triangles (`Uncertain` past either). Ray parity against the other shells' meshes (exact orientation tests; a point within the chord and tolerances decides nothing, one in a triangle's plane further off misses it; a shell with no vertices uses a mesh point) says which encloses which: even depth is a solid's outer shell, odd a cavity of the innermost; shells partly inside each other are `ShellsCross`. A pair no vertex decides (every vertex of one within the chord of the other: a lens a few hundredths of a millimetre inside its can) is judged again on a mesh with an eighth of the chord, up to `ROUNDS` (5) times and down to ten resolutions, before it is `Uncertain` (#317; the first round is the old one, so only what was refused changes). Those finer meshes cost work units: they push KiCad's two Keystone battery holders past ryucad's default heal budget (#398, see `ryucad-step.md`'s Heal budgets). Shells facing the wrong way are turned over and the body built again with these solids, whatever the file said about voids.
- **Tightening** (`heal::tighten`). An edge more than resolution off its two faces, both analytic or pipe, takes the branch of their intersection (`surface_surface` in its box) that crosses non-tangentially, is closer to both surfaces than the edge, and holds 17 samples and both vertices within its reach (twice its gap, at least ten resolutions, at most the cap), from its vertices' feet the way the edge ran at its middle (reversed, uses and all, where the branch runs the other way). Vertices stay; the file's pcurves for it are dropped. A closed edge whose vertex lies within that reach of a periodic branch's start takes the branch's whole range, where its clamped pcurves fit (#373). An edge whose middle's foot on a face's surface with no implicit form (a spline, a swept surface) lies within its reach of an end of a direction that surface's domain bounds is tolerated untraced (v0.8.2, #394, #239): the faces meet along that domain edge, where no trace certifies a crossing, so the intersector traced the whole box (metres for the sewing race's bodies) and again on the surfaces carried past their edges, seconds to minutes an edge, and on the race, the Switchwire's inserts and the corpora none of those edges was tightened but nist_ctc_02's AP242 edition's four a body (plane × spline, now tolerated at 2.7 µm). On cox-worker-1 (Xeon E5-2650, one thread a process) the race's ten slowest bodies sewed in 87.5 s, from 895.7 s, bit for bit the same, and the Switchwire's M3 insert healed in 167 s, from 806 s.
- **Gaps and pcurves.** Tolerances grow to cover each edge's distance from its faces (certified on analytic surfaces) and each vertex's from its edges' ends and faces; past the cap is `GapTooLarge`. A fitted pcurve aims at twice the larger of the edge's tolerance and gap when either is measurable, else at resolution. A fit starting or ending at a pole reads the parameters' derivative a millionth of the way in, else four times as far, up to a sixteenth (on a 10 µm arc a millionth in still lies within resolution of the pole, whose projection has no derivative: ST's NUCLEO board's corner patches, #434). A fit's steps are kept under a quarter period by the samples' derivatives as well as by their unwrapped parameters, which see a step only modulo the period; a spline edge that four samples a piece don't settle is fitted again at sixteen (#320: KiCad's Würth inductors wind a helix of up to 28 turns, in 60° rational arcs). A use no such fit certifies is fitted once more aiming at a hundred resolutions, its certified bound (at most `1e-5` m) the edge's tolerance (#240: the Switchwire's Revo toolhead, a 78 µm line from a spline row whose `v` speed nearly vanishes, where the bound stalls at 3.5e-7 m however finely the spans are halved); only uses healing refused before take it. In the build an edge whose curve ends off its vertices has a tolerance of its own plus that gap, so its pcurves' images end within it (the mesher measures them so).
- **Collapsing** (`heal::collapse`). An open edge whose length bound (a line's or arc's length, a spline's hull) is within its tolerance goes, its vertices joined (`Merged`), unless that would close another edge or empty a loop. A face whose one loop is two open edges between the same vertices within their tolerance of each other (17 samples) goes and its edges join into one (`Merged`) used by the faces either side; narrower than the cap but wider than the tolerance, it is `SliverTooWide` when its surface coincides with a neighbouring face's, and otherwise a thin face of the model, kept (#201); wider, it is the model's.
- **Report and journal.** `HealReport` lists `sewn`, `merged`, `collapsed`, `seams`, `poles`, `joined`, `reversed`, `turned` (by neighbours or by their shell; turned twice is as the file had it), `tightened`, `tolerances` and `approximated`; the journal has `Merged` for joins, `Deleted` for seams, poles and collapsed entities, `Modified` for reversed, turned and tightened ones, and `Created` for everything built.
- **Testing and limits.** Unit tests per step (`heal/tests.rs`: sewing, loops, faces, shells inside out, cavities facing out or misdeclared, a solid inside a cavity, crossing shells, tightened rims and lines, short edges, slivers both ways); `tests/heal_props.rs`'s `faults_heal_or_are_refused` (400 seeds of `ryukei_testkit::heal_bytes`: faults healing must undo give the body's topology back, others a body that checks or a refusal); the `heal` fuzz target (about 3 to 8 runs a second), its finds replayed in `tests/fuzz_regressions.rs`. Tightening never moves vertices; since plan 8.C it takes any pair of faces (see Booleans and healing on every surface below). 7.D added T-junctions, overlapping faces and edges through poles (see Real-world files below).

## Real-world files (decided in phase 7)

The design is document 55 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 7.D.

- **Corpus.** `crates/ryustep/tests/corpus` holds ten of NIST's MBE PMI test models (AP203, geometry only; public domain), kept byte for byte (`binary` in `.gitattributes`), and `manifest.txt`: each body healed, with OCCT's volume of its solid, or refused (their PMI sheets are open shells until phase 9). `tests/corpus.rs` checks it in every CI job. `pinned.txt` lists the pinned corpus by URL and SHA-256 (NIST's whole package, OCCT 7.8.1's two samples) and how many bodies each file heals; `scripts/fetch-corpus.sh` fetches it into `target/corpus`, never committed; the ABC dataset's archive was unreachable when this was planned (HTTP 500). A file healing fewer bodies than recorded fails `ryukei-bench`'s `step` test; when a change heals more, raise the count.
- **References.** `ryukei-bench`'s `occt` shim reads STEP (`STEPControl_Reader`, then `ShapeFix_Shape`), writes it (`STEPControl_Writer`) and measures each solid; the devcontainer installs OCCT's data exchange package. `tests/step.rs`: every body ryustep heals from the corpus and the OCCT files encloses one of OCCT's solids' volumes within its mesh's error; OCCT reads every file ryustep writes from seeded bodies, a solid per lump, of the body's volume. SMLib has no STEP reader in usd-brep's sources and takes no part.
- **The race.** 1,600 seeded parts, each placed three times (123 MB of AP242 in millimetres), read, healed and written by ryustep on all cores against OCCT reading, fixing and writing it: ryukei must be faster (80.6 s against 99.5 s when planned). `benches/step.rs` times parsing, decoding, reading and writing separately.
- **Reading.** A missing reference direction is x, or y where the axis is along x or within rounding of it; a line's, plane's or cylinder's origin outside the size box slides to the foot of the world origin; `AUTOMOTIVE_DESIGN_CC1` is AP214. Representations holding the same items in one context are read once. `Imported::instances` maps every import name to the STEP instance it came from (`heal::import_names`), so a refusal can be found in the file. Writing cuts bodies open in parallel.
  - a cone whose radius is within resolution of zero is read at its apex (#326);
  - an extrusion of a straight curve (a line, or a spline whose control points lie within resolution of one) is the plane through the curve and the direction (#314);
  - a closed direction that is one Bézier piece is refined by a knot before it is made periodic (#312);
  - an unclamped periodic spline repeats `degree` control points as ryukei writes it, else, at a joint of multiplicity `k`, `degree + 1 - k` as OCCT and KiCad write it, and is read periodic either way (#325, #403; OCCT's `nurbs_cylinder.stp` rims among them);
  - a horn torus (radii equal within resolution) is the revolution of its tube circle with a gap of 100 resolutions where it touches the axis, since a curve may meet the axis only at an angle (#311);
  - a `SURFACE_OF_REVOLUTION` of a circle in a plane through its axis and touching it is that horn torus (#316);
  - one of a circle crossing its axis, which as a whole folds through the axis, is read per face (`Geo::crossing_revolution`): the revolution of the one arc between the crossings that the face's vertices and points along its edges lie on, over the stretch they span and half the way on to each crossing, so it stays clear of the axis (#316: KiCad's axial inductors end on arcs of circles hundreds of millimetres across); a face whose points lie on both arcs (KiCad's HC33-U crystals, which wrap round the profile through the axis) stays refused `Degenerate`;
- **Healing.** Sewing splits a free edge where another face's free vertex lies on it (a T-junction), and drops a short free edge whose ends sewing joined; an edge through a pole or apex of a face's surface is split there (`HealReport::split`). Faces on coincident surfaces overlapping in an area are `HealError::FacesOverlap` (`check::check_overlaps`; `check_intersections` also takes faces whose boundaries coincide all round). An exact or file pcurve missing its edge's ends by more than resolution is mapped again through them; where the edge is a spline or the surface isn't analytic it is screened at eight samples per knot span and per 64th, and past the tolerance a line or polynomial spline is certified (`intersect::pcurve_certified`, raising the tolerance up to ten times) or fitted. Spline surfaces bound their first derivatives (`NurbsSurface::first_bound`), so fits on them certify. Tightening skips faces within a tenth of a radian of tangent at the edge's middle (a hundredth until v0.8.2: spline faces of Altium and ST board exports meeting at 1° to 4° spent 20 to 470 s an edge tracing and tightened none, and every edge the corpora tighten meets at a sine of 0.15 or more; #279, #434); shell facing meshes at a hundredth of the body's size and a right angle. A representation's solids that touch (#315, #208), cross (#319, plan 14.C) or leave which encloses which uncertain (#317) are each healed alone and united with one boolean (ryustep, `read::united`); where any of that fails the refusal stands. Shells crossing within one solid of the file, or its faces overlapping, are repaired by healing (`HealOptions::repair`, see `kernel-tolerant.md`'s Self-union).
- **Limits.** A spindle torus (minor radius over major) is `InvalidGeometry` (OCCT's `screw.step`); a face on a revolution of a circle crossing its axis that uses both of its arcs is refused (KiCad's HC33-U crystals, #316); left by release 0.7.3 for 0.8.x: #313, #316, #320, #323, #364, #401, #402, #417, and #337 (mass) for ryukei 15.B; two of 1,600 seeded bodies don't come back through STEP (`Uncertain`, a pipe's tube); surface models are refused as open shells unless sheets are asked for (`Open::Sheet`, plan 12.C: see `kernel-sheets.md`).

## Offset surfaces in STEP (decided in phase 8)

The design is document 94 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.D.

- `OFFSET_SURFACE` of a B-spline, an extrusion or a revolution reads as an `Offset` over its basis's whole domain (no longer `NotBounded`), refused as `InvalidGeometry { Degenerate }` where it folds (OCCT's `offset_sheet.stp` offsets a sheet curving to a 0.57 mm radius by 1 mm); an `Offset` face is written as `OFFSET_SURFACE` of its base, exactly. STEP offsets along `σ_u × σ_v`, and a surface of revolution runs on its curve first, so its distance is negated both ways.
- A face on an offset that wraps a periodic base gets its seams fitted along the offset's parameter lines (`intersect::fit_along`), each seam edge's tolerance the fit's certified bound: an offset spline cylinder's side and an offset spline torus go through STEP and back (`tests/write.rs`). Writing drops the offset's box and reading takes the base's whole domain, so a face on an offset certified only over its own box reads back `Degenerate` where the whole base folds.
