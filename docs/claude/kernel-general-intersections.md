## General intersections (decided in phase 8)

The design is document 94 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.A.

- **Surfaces over jets.** `geom::Sheet` takes every surface: a NURBS by the hull of its Bézier patches' enclosures (`NurbsSurface::jet_box`, sharing the mesher's patch arithmetic, so mesh bounds keep their bits), an extrusion and a revolution by their curve's (`Curve::jet_box`). `intersect::cells` gives any surface's first cells near a box: patches, curve pieces, eighths of a turn, and the range a box reaches along an unbounded direction.
- **Curve–surface.** `curve_surface` meets a NURBS, extrusion or revolution by subdivision of `(t, u, v)` (`intersect::sheet_cs`): boxes excluded by enclosures, crossings by Krawczyk's test, contacts certified along a path that follows the curve to second order (a curve lying on a surface needs boxes only as short as curvature allows), a tangency the one root of `((C - S) · S_u, (C - S) · S_v, C' · N)`. Hits follow the analytic rules.
- **One implicit form.** Any other surface against an analytic one is traced in its own parameters against the analytic implicit form (`intersect::sheet_trace`, the pipe tracer generalized: a pipe's grid as before, bit for bit; periodic directions in sixteenths, an extrusion's height over the range the region reaches), cut at a revolution's poles and a NURBS's collapsed rows (`NurbsSurface::collapsed_rows`), and fitted with that surface read through its signed distance from its foot.
- **No implicit form.** Two such surfaces are traced in their four parameters (`intersect::general`): pairs of cells excluded (boxes apart, or a component of `E = A - B`, or `n · E` along either cell's middle normal, of one sign by the mean value form), simple (a component of `N_A × N_B` of one sign: a regular curve, monotone along it, so no loop hides) or singular. A simple pair's crossings on its eight faces are solved by Krawczyk's test and matched across neighbours by their certified boxes; a pair holding more than two is split again; a singular cluster is settled by one certified point of tangency (`((A - B) · B_s, (A - B) · B_t, N_B · A_u, N_B · A_v) = 0`, regular at a touch where the squared distance's system is not). Branches on two surfaces with no implicit form are fitted with a local foot and certified by a pcurve bound that encloses `d²S(P) - C''` as a vector (`fit::sheet_pcurve_bound`), so the curvatures cancel.
- **Tangency along a curve.** Past 20 splits a pair where Gauss-Newton steps on the tangency system rest inside it, within the touch reach, is kept whole. A cluster with no single certified point is marched along the system's null direction (Newton on three of its equations, the fastest parameter held) until it closes, leaves a domain's edge or the region; every part of its first surface's cells outside a strip `sqrt(2 × size × reach)` round the curve is certified apart from the second (`general::apart`), and no transversal arc may end in it. It becomes a tangent branch, fitted along its pcurve on the first surface (`fit_along`) and certified against the second up to the reach. `surface_surface` tries this path where the implicit one is uncertain.
- **Poles.** Along a spline's row collapsed to one point exactly, `S_u` vanishes, so `S_u = (v - v₀) M` with `M` enclosed by `S_uv` over the cell stretched to the row: the four-parameter trace judges a pair near a pole simple by `M × S_v` as well as by `S_u × S_v`, and fitting reads a foot at a pole by the spline's limit normal, a local foot that doesn't settle going to projection (issue #128). `general_props` converts spheres whole, poles and all.
- **Coincidence.** `intersect::coincide` also names equal surfaces; a NURBS (or a revolution by its exact NURBS form) certified patch by patch within resolution of an analytic implicit form; an extrusion along a cylinder's axis whose curve the cylinder's form certifies; two NURBS whose nets agree after one is turned, mapped affinely onto the other's domain and both refined to each other's knots; and a NURBS that is such a part of another. `surface_surface` asks it before any path.
- **Self-intersection check.** `face_face` takes every pair (the patch-sign test first, as a fast way apart). `beside` takes a face on any surface; another surface with no implicit form has no side, so every part outside the strip is certified apart from it instead. A rail's 256 pieces are halved where they lie wider across both parameters than half the strip, so rails off the parameter lines cover the parts beside them. Faces touching at a vertex they share touch at a corner of both faces' boxes, where the region's sides cut the trace through the touch: a pair that fails there is intersected again in a region reaching an eighth of the smaller face's size past it (`face_face_within`). Coincident faces place an edge that can't be fitted onto the first surface (a tolerant edge read from a file) by its own pcurve, when its face is on that very surface. Swept and NURBS bodies check with no `CannotCheck`, as does every body healed from the OCCT files and the committed corpus (since plan 8.F, see below).
- **Booleans.** Since plan 8.C the table takes faces on every surface (see Booleans and healing on every surface below).
- **Testing and limits.** Unit tests per module; `tests/intersect_props.rs` (curves against the analytic surfaces' exact NURBS forms agree with the implicit path; issue #83's reproducer); `tests/general_props.rs` (release only, its own CI step: spline–analytic and spline–spline pairs against sampling, splines touching along a line and a circle); the `general` fuzz target; golden hashes (`general` section). A fitted span's curvature in `fit::pcurve_bound` is now scaled to the span's parameter (it was understated by `w²` after halving). Limits: a curve along a bounded surface's domain edge (a ruled chamfer's end ruling lying on its end face) is `Uncertain`; a pair of splines takes about 0.1 to 1 s, a tangency round a whole circle about 10 s (NURBS enclosures and the strip's certification dominate); `beside` against a surface with no implicit form is 20 to 40 times slower than against an implicit one.

## Booleans and healing on every surface (decided in phase 8)

The design is document 94 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.C.

- **Booleans.** The table takes every pair of faces, on any surfaces: `face_face`, edge hits, touches and triple points no longer leave out NURBS, swept and pipe faces. `BooleanError::Unsupported` means only surfaces no intersector takes. Splitting takes a NURBS's or a revolution's collapsed rows as poles (`split::Node::Pole` with its free parameter either way, the turn read from how the row's neighbours circle it, the limit normal or at an apex the mean of the normals beside it), and bands close where a row collapses.
- **Face boxes.** A NURBS, revolution or sweep face's box is its surface's enclosure over the face's parameter box (`intersect::face::face_rect`: pcurves cut to their ranges with `NurbsCurve2::clipped_bounds`, a periodic direction whole, on to a collapsed end where the other direction is periodic), grown by its edges' tolerances (`intersect::cells::enclosure`). Healing keeps the old box (`coarse_box`) to size its shell meshes, so what it makes doesn't change.
- **Splines that close up.** A NURBS periodic both ways closes up with genus 1, periodic one way with both ends of the other collapsed with genus 0 (`NurbsSurface::closed_genus`), so a face on it may have no loops: a closed tube reads back from STEP, and a closed loft of one closed curve builds as one face (`LoftError::ClosedTube` is gone).
- **Poles.** Where the other surface (or a curve) passes within the touch reach (100 resolutions) of a collapsed row, the strip beside it over which the surface lies within half the reach of the pole is a zone: `sheet_trace` leaves it whole and ends what crosses its side at the pole (a settled cluster), `sheet_cs` leaves it out and meets the curve at the pole. Sides at a pole read the limit normal; cutting a branch already ending at a pole leaves no empty piece.
- **Seams and knot lines.** The intersectors' retries cut their first cells into equal parts moved off the surface's pieces (`intersect::cells::cells_off`), the curve–surface search its curve's sixteenths too, and the four-parameter trace moves and splits its second surface at the complementary fraction: a plane through a spline sphere's equator, a line crossing a periodic seam, and two sweeps along one path no longer put the curve on a cell's side.
- **Faces apart.** When the surfaces' trace stays `Uncertain`, `face_face` certifies the two faces apart over their own parameter boxes (`beside::faces_apart`: `general::apart_within`, parts certified outside either face left out by `beside`'s test), and surfaces that meet nowhere in the region need no edge cuts. A curve stretch whose `E` lies within the tolerance over a whole box is a contact where a path can't be followed (a curve ending at a domain corner); each such contact costs 1/128 of the search's box budget, so a curve on a surface whose path can't be bounded ends uncertain after at most 128 of them, not after the whole budget (the booleans and sweeps tests take 8 at most).
- **Domain edges.** A trace or a curve–surface search still `Uncertain` is tried once more on its surfaces carried past their bounded domain edges, exactly: a NURBS's end pieces' own polynomials in Bézier form (`NurbsSurface::extended`, `NurbsCurve::extended`, its knots over its own domain kept bit for bit), a sweep along its profile (its path can't be carried on without changing its frame), an extrusion's or revolution's curve short of the axis, by a sixteenth and three thirty-seconds of the end pieces so the carried edges don't end together. `face_face` tries it after certifying faces apart, and never for faces sharing an edge or a vertex (the check settles those by `beside`); hits on a carried surface must lie on the surface itself, and the public `surface_surface` clips each branch it finds there to the stretches whose pcurves lie in both surfaces' domains (`inside_runs`). A loft standing on a block, and a ruled chamfer ending on a face, build.
- **Coincident splines.** Two flat NURBS on one plane are coincident (`coincide::planar`: every control point within half the resolution of the plane through the net's corner); coincident faces' rims are worked out in the parameters of a surface holding both (`coincide::common`: the analytic one, the plane, or the spline the other is part of); a degree 1 spline pcurve of two control points is met as its line in `intersect2::crossings`; and faces either side of an edge merge onto a surface holding both (`coincide::covering`, or the common plane) or stay apart. A tolerant edge read from a file takes a pcurve fitted at twice its tolerance or gap when its face merges.
- **Healing.** Tightening takes any pair of faces: a closed edge takes a closed clamped branch started at its vertex's foot (`intersect::reseamed`, moved from `blend`), and a tightened edge's uses take the branch's own pcurves where their maps are the identity.
- **The mesh estimate.** A cell whose estimate passes 4,096 triangles is halved both ways while its quarters come to less than half of it, five times at most: a sweep turned to a tool's place, bounded in axes it lies askew to, was refused as `MeshTooLarge`. The estimate only gates, so no mesh changes.
- **Testing.** `tests/general_booleans.rs` (spline plates and rods, lofts drilled, a spline ball capped, halved through its poles and cut to an octant, a twisted bar through a torus (release only), a twisted loft and a loft standing on a block, flush spline blocks, neighbouring loft sides; its own CI step); `tests/general_boolean_props.rs` (48 seeded pairs of spline, swept and lofted bodies against each other and primitives, a third placed square to the axes, drawn from a fixed seed so the release gate can't flip; release only, its own CI step); ryustep's `tests/drill.rs` (bodies through STEP drilled and halved; NIST CTC 05 drilled through a spline fillet, release only, its own CI step); unit tests per module; the `general_booleans` golden section; the `boolean` fuzz target's general case (`ryukei_testkit::boolean_bytes::general_case`, an odd first byte).
- **Limits.** #133 (the check's cost on thin sweep ribbons) is plan 8.F's round closed paths (open ones are judged one to one first, see Sweeps along paths and lofts; #128 is settled under Poles above); two splines that only overlap (neither flat on one plane nor a part of the other) aren't coincident, so their faces stay `Uncertain`; tangencies the grid placement makes between a spline and an analytic surface can leave `split` uncertain (a typed refusal).

## References, the race and hardening (decided in phase 8)

The design is document 94 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.F.

- **Fits on every surface.** A traced branch's pcurve on a surface with third derivatives (a NURBS, an extrusion, a revolution, a sweep) is certified by Taylor's formula to third order about each span's middle (`fit::third_order`): the error and its first two derivatives at the middle (evaluated, with a part in 10⁹ of their terms for rounding, as the bound's other terms take) and its third over the span (`Sheet::jet3_box` and the chain rule), so a span the fit follows to resolution certifies with few halvings or none. Offsets keep the second-order bound. Where both bounds certify a span the fit makes the same samples and tolerance, so no golden hash moved; the race's pins went from about 15 enclosures a span to 1.5.
- **Enclosures.** A NURBS's patch enclosures over a box cut their Bézier nets in place, by the same de Casteljau arithmetic in the same order (`bound::sub_curve_in`, `left_in`, `right_in`), so the same bits without allocating (a quarter off every spline intersection).
- **Edge cuts.** A pair of faces cuts each face's edges with the other's surface only where an edge's box meets a branch's (grown by their tolerances): a cut splits only a branch it lies within reach of, so nothing changes but the time (a block's long edges against each of a thousand pins).
- **Coincident faces.** A closed edge no cut divides is a rim all round (it was skipped as a piece whose ends meet), and a face on a copy of a surface of revolution turned about its own axis (two copies of one torus placed differently in a file) takes its own pcurve onto the other copy, moved along `u` by the turn (`face::turned`).
- **The boolean table and splitting.** Pieces of carriers known to run together are one global edge when each one's middle lies on the other piece (a traced stretch along a lofted rim runs on parameters of its own, so their middles needn't meet); the two sides of a closed stretch, walked each way across a band, take one height (their crossings of the line across came out an ulp apart); and the implicit trace pairs its singular leaves by a sweep along `s`, not two by two, the same clusters.
- **Layout.** A face whose loops the build left on several faces, two on one and two on another, gathers them: a holder gives up its loops but one as faces of their own (MFKRH), each joining the kept face (KFMRH), a face whose loops can't move yet (moving one would split the shell) waiting for the others. The build's order comes from names, so healing a file depended on its import key (issue #234: 60 of 4,000 keys failed a block with three holes).
- **Near-square sections.** A plane within resolution of square to a cylinder's axis (the cosine rounding to 1 or past it) cuts a circle of radius `r` in the plane, its gap the edge's tolerance (issue #157: the swapped ellipse was refused and the near-axis spline path cut a rim off the cylinder).
- **The corpus.** Every body healed from the OCCT files and the committed corpus checks clean, `nist_ctc_05` included (its 25 pairs: about three minutes in release, from twenty), and `nist_ctc_04`, `nist_ftc_07` and `nist_ftc_10` take seconds (from minutes each), so CI checks them all. `ryukei-bench`'s `step` test checks every body the pinned corpus heals, nothing left unchecked but 11 listed pairs in the AP242 editions of three NIST cases whose AP203 files check clean (issue #264: a new pair fails, a listed one that clears is printed); the five files whose checks take over a minute (`linkrods.step` 44 minutes, `nist_ctc_02`'s AP242 edition 20 to 25) run by name (`pinned_slow_files_check_clean`). It drills a fixed set of them and fillets each one's longest straight edge between planes with its tangent chain, halving the radius where the faces round it can't take it and trying the next longest edge where none will do; `nist_ctc_04`'s AP242 edition refuses its five longest at every radius (`Overflow` down to 0.6 mm, issue #265) and is listed, and `nist_ftc_06` is taken in its AP203 edition (#264).
- **The race.** A block whose top is a lofted bicubic face (`ryukei_testkit::nurbs_race`), minus 1,000 pins lofted through wavy closed cubics, on one thread against OCCT's `BRepAlgoAPI_Cut`. Design 94 asked ryukei to beat OCCT; plan 8.F ships it at about twice OCCT's time, and beating OCCT is phase 9's (#254). `ryukei-bench`'s `nurbs` test asserts ryukei within two and a half times OCCT's time, a guard against regressions (OCCT's own time ranged from 44.9 to 75.4 s across runs, which twice left no margin for), and prints the ratio, SMLib's time and the volumes. On cox-worker-2: ryukei about 360 s at 8.E, 97.9 to 98.0 s after this plan (the fit's bound, enclosures cut in place, edges cut only near a branch) against OCCT's 44.9 s, 2.18 times, in two runs on 2026-10-30. An earlier "win" against OCCT's 183 s was against pins OCCT misread (see STEP below). Pins standing flush on the block's bottom (#77 on spline faces) take about 18 to 21 times the clear pins' time per pin (an edge lying in a face, a trace along a domain edge); OCCT cuts a row of them in 3 to 8 s. SMLib, recorded beside them, takes 133 to 178 s.
- **References.** `ryukei-bench`'s `occt` shim lofts (`BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections`), sweeps (`MakePipeShell`), offsets (`MakeOffsetShape`), hollows (`MakeThickSolid`) and intersects faces (`BRepAlgoAPI_Section`: `GeomAPI_IntSS` on the bare rational surfaces gave curves off them); SMLib runs through its Python (`tests/nurbs/reference.py`) on ryuformats' USD. Every exact volume agrees; OCCT's volumes of rational NURBS (its `GProp`) are off by up to 10%, so they are printed, never judged; where both references agree and ryukei doesn't, the test fails.
- **Testing and limits.** Unit tests (the in-place subdivision's bits, the third-order bound against samples, turned copies), `tests/general_booleans.rs` (a closed rim, a flush pin, pin 695), `tests/booleans.rs` (#157), `tests/heal_props.rs` (#234 over 4,000 keys), `tests/snug_fits.rs` (issue #159's figures under rounded poses, clear or touching: not reproduced), `tests/fuzz_regressions.rs` (the campaign's two finds), the `nurbs_race` golden section, `benches/general.rs`, `loft.rs` and `offset.rs`, and the `general` and `heal` fuzz targets in CI. Left for phase 9: a sweep along an open path shrunk (#223: its sides' offsets end together at the path's ends, where neither the trace nor `beside`'s tangent strip settles faces meeting at an angle); pipes on curved splines meeting in a ball corner (#177); the blend campaign's spline fillet whose pipe face the mesher stalls on (#255); beating OCCT in the race, and flush pins' cost (#254).
