## Geometry (decided in phase 2)

The design is document 17 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`.

- **Types.** `geom::Curve` (line, circle, ellipse, NURBS), `geom::Surface` (plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, NURBS) and the pcurve type `geom::Curve2` (line, circle, ellipse, NURBS in a surface's `(u, v)` plane) are `#[non_exhaustive]` enums, `Clone` but not `Copy`. Analytic variants are fast paths with closed forms. A NURBS sits behind an `Arc` and never changes once built, so cloning a body shares it; its Bézier pieces are built on first use in a `OnceLock` that takes no part in equality or the dump. Fins carry pcurves (see Pcurves and cavities below).
- **NURBS.** Rational, degree 1 to 25, with a `KnotVector` that is clamped or periodic (distinct knots plus multiplicities). Constructors reject bad input with `Error::InvalidNurbs(NurbsError)`, `NonFinite` or `OutsideSizeBox`; collapsed control rows (poles) are allowed. `refine`, `split` and `to_clamped` return clamped splines; `transformed` takes a `Rigid`. Every analytic type converts exactly (`to_nurbs`), with a `Reparam` relating the parameters.
- **Derivatives.** `eval` gives derivatives up to second order. Tangents and normals are `Option`: a NURBS judges vanishing on derivatives scaled to its domain, and gives the limit normal at a pole. Principal curvatures are measured against the natural normal, positive where the surface bends towards it.
- **Projection.** `project` returns a point within `LINEAR_RESOLUTION` of the true global minimum, and among minima that close, the lowest parameter. The search works on the Bernstein coefficients of `(S - p)·S'` per Bézier piece: one sign means no stationary point, one sign change means exactly one (curves); surfaces add an interval Newton step and a depth limit, then Newton's method. Domain boundaries, kinks and creases (knots of multiplicity `degree`) are searched as curves, and every candidate must be a genuine local minimum. Every crease's feet are found first, and with pruning only creases whose feet lie within resolution of the nearest point so far are judged (each judgement splits the surface there; a ruled chamfer fitted to resolution has hundreds of creases, and projecting onto one took seconds), which changes no result. Degenerate splines (folds, collapsed patches, poles) are where this breaks: the property tests and the `nurbs` fuzz target pin the cases found so far.
- **Bodies.** Euler operators, the checker, `compact` and the dump accept every variant. The checker measures distances through `project`, bounds NURBS by their hulls, requires edge intervals inside a clamped curve's domain, and lets a closed edge lie on a clamped curve whose ends meet. A face with no loops is still only allowed on an analytic sphere or torus.

## Offset surfaces (decided in phase 8)

The design is document 94 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.D.

- **Type.** `Surface::Offset(Arc<Offset>)` is `S(u, v) + d N(u, v)` over a box of its base's parameters, the base a NURBS, an extrusion or a revolution (analytic surfaces and pipes offset to their own kinds; an offset of an offset is one offset by the distances' sum, over the boxes' overlap). It keeps its base's parameters, so a pcurve on the base is one on the offset. `Offset::new` certifies it regular over the box in interval arithmetic: `1 - d κ` positive for both principal curvatures (their product and sum, from the fundamental forms, `> 1e-9` of their scale), over the base's own cells cut to the box, halved up to 16 times (16,384 cells), each cell's enclosures taken in the frame of its middle's tangent plane, and a revolution over one angle and an extrusion over one height (their curvatures don't depend on them); a pole or fold is `Degenerate`. Its natural normal is then its base's.
- **Arms.** Third derivatives of NURBS (`NurbsSurface::jet3`, `jet3_box`, the Leibniz quotient over Bézier patches), extrusions, revolutions and (since plan 8.E) sweeps (`Sheet::jets3_at`, `jet3_box`; see Offset bodies and shells) give the offset's jets (`offset::offset_jets`, the normal from `S_u` and `S_v` as jets), so it is a `Sheet` and every intersector takes it; enclosures over a wide box are the hull over its cells, halved up to six times. Projection is `sheet::nearest` from its cells; derivative bounds from its jets per cell; `transformed` and `mirrored` (the distance negated where the mirror flips the base's normal); codec tag 10 (surfaces nest at most eight deep when read); `to_nurbs` is `Degenerate`, and `to_nurbs_within` (plan 8.E) gives a certified bicubic Hermite form for export (see Offset bodies and shells). A box of no width one way (a parameter line) takes the cells holding it, so a fit along a seam's line on a cell's side is bounded; and a pcurve bound that comes out NaN (an infinite derivative bound times no velocity) counts as infinite, never as passing. `Surface::offset(d)` gives an `Offset` over the whole domain for those bases and sweeps, `offset_within(d, rect)` over a face's box; it is `Some` for every surface.
- **Pipes on rails.** `Pipe::on_rail(spine, r, anchor, pcurve, map)` anchors a pipe on any surface: `e₁` points from the spine to the rail `S(p(m₀ + m₁ s))`, made perpendicular to the tangent, so the frame still depends on `s` alone; the direction to the rail is enclosed by the mean value form about each stretch's middle (the rail and the spine move together), and an enclosure over a long stretch is the hull of its halves' (up to six times). Codec tag 11; pipes on analytic anchors keep tag 8 and their bits.
