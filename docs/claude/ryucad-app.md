## Desktop app (decided in ryucad phase 1)

The design is document 28 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; the plan is the phase 1.B plan there.

- **Process.** `ryucad-app` is eframe (egui on winit and wgpu), all Rust. It is a client of the project's daemon and nothing else: it depends on the `ryucad` library for `proto` and `client`, never touches project files (a damaged project is repaired by running `ryucad doctor`) and never calls ryukei. It starts daemons from the `ryucad` binary beside it, else on `PATH`, which must be the same build: the same ryucad, ryukei, commit and protocol (`VersionInfo::same_build`; the target differs on Linux, where the app is glibc and the CLI static musl). `RYUCAD_APP` names the app for `ryucad open`.
- **Connections.** Two per window, as one human participant named `<OS user> (window n)`: requests on a thread, notifications on a `client::Subscription`, which wakes its own reader when dropped so a closed window leaves the session. A lost daemon greys the window, which reconnects with backoff and loads again.
- **Model.** Parts and features from `rebuild` reports (which give each feature's `type`), meshes fetched as `ryucad.mesh.1` one body per request and kept by body hash, so an unchanged body is never fetched twice. Cameras move with `view::{orbit, pan, zoom}` and are shared at most every 150 ms; matrices are made relative to a reference point near the model so f32 GPU positions stay precise.
- **Drawing.** The scene renders with wgpu into a texture of its own (MSAA when the adapter can), shown as an egui image and read back in tests; each body uploads once per hash, and selections, highlights and hover paint over faces from a per-frame buffer at equal depth. Picking is a CPU ray cast on faces and screen distance for edges and vertices (vertex over edge over face, nothing hidden). Edges are pulled towards the eye by 0.1% of their distance, along their sight line, and the near plane stays at least far/1000, so hidden edges stay hidden close up; egui samples a plain (not sRGB) view of the scene's sRGB texture, so the window shows the colours drawn. The window's look is the `ui.style` preference (`drafting`, the default, `graphite` or `studio`; `ui::style::Look`): predefined palettes, corners and spacing for egui's light and dark styles, the same layout in each, no shadows, and `ui.theme` picks the mode. The viewport's background is the style's in a light theme and one shared grey in a dark one (the styles' dark viewports sit too close to edges). Picking judges an edge's visibility at its own pixel, with a perspective-correct point. Navigation: an orbit turns about the model under the press (else the middle of the view, else the model's middle); the wheel zooms towards the model under the cursor, over the background towards the cursor at the depth of the model's middle, and zooming at a point puts the target on the view axis at that point's depth, so it stays by the model. On a trackpad (scrolls in points not from a wheel) two fingers pan, Shift+two fingers orbit, a pinch zooms; on macOS Ctrl+click is a right-click and the SolidWorks pan is Cmd+middle. Hardware adapters first, else a software one (lavapipe, WARP) with a note in the status line; an adapter without what the scene needs (`VIEW_FORMATS`, which OpenGL drivers often lack) is passed over, and Vulkan, Metal or DX12 come before OpenGL. On X11 the app turns off the server's input method (`XMODIFIERS=@im=none`; `RYUCAD_XIM=1` keeps it), since through XIM fast typing lost keys and moved spaces; a window a DPI change left past its monitor is shrunk and moved back onto it. On Wayland `ryucad open` asks for attention (a window can't bring itself forward there), and the title bar is winit's Adwaita one, with the title. Showing another part (View > Show, `ryucad focus`) frames it as the first view does (#211); View > Show's parts scroll past the window's height (#209). A solid none of whose chain built says only `failed (code)` (#213).
- **Occurrences selected.** A selection or highlight naming an occurrence (`<asm>/<path>`) paints every face of every solid it places, and Zoom to selection frames them (`Model::occurrence_bodies`); the BOM tab selects lines' occurrences this way (see ryupdm.md's BOM tab).
- **Writes.** The property panel saves through `feature.edit` against the revision `feature.show` read it at (`feature.show` now returns it); a conflict reloads the form and lists what changed. Settings go through `config.set`; preference writes hold a lock file, since every project's daemon shares `preferences.json`, and the daemon (not the app) puts a project first among the recent ones when a window says hello.
- **Tests.** App tests run against real daemons, building `ryucad` themselves; drawing tests skip without an adapter unless `RYUCAD_REQUIRE_GPU` is set (CI sets it on Linux, with lavapipe, and Windows). Window tests drive the real app with `egui_kittest`, whose accessibility check means every control needs a name. The viewport snapshot is lavapipe's, compared within a tolerance.
- **Packaging.** Release archives carry `ryucad` and `ryucad-app` (plus `share/` files on Linux and macOS); installers install both unless `RYUCAD_NO_APP=1`, and register "Open in ryucad" on folders (Windows, per user), a desktop entry and `ryucad.json` MIME type (Linux; not folders, which would make it the default for every folder on a desktop with none, such as Xfce), and an AppleScript launcher compiled at install time into `~/Applications/ryucad.app` (macOS, unsigned).

## Measure in the app (decided in ryucad phase 10)

The design is document 198 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its phase 2, plan 10.A.

- **Measure tool.** Tools > Measure turns the tool on (`core::measure::Tool`): while on, a click on a face, edge or vertex is measured, not selected; a second pick measures the pair, a third (or the same one again) starts over. Each pick sends `measure` with the picks (`Pending::Measure`), and an answer for picks that have moved on is dropped. The Measure window lists the answer's values (`(exact)` or `± bound` in SI units) with Copy (the picks, then a line per value); between two entities the viewport draws a dimension line between the nearest points with the distance as its label. Esc, or closing the window, leaves the tool, before Esc clears the selection. The picks are measured again when their part changes.
- **Mass properties.** Tools > Mass properties weighs the solid or occurrence of the first selected entity (`core::measure::weighed`), else the one part or assembly shown, and lists mass, volume, area, the centre of mass and the principal moments, each part's material and mass, and the warnings; the viewport marks the centre of mass. Weigh again takes the selection anew; the panel weighs again when its part changes.
- **Clearance.** Tools > Clearance lists the shown assembly's pairs closest first, a pair at 0 marked as touching or interfering, each with Show, which frames both nearest points (`View::fit_points`) and draws the line between them; it asks again when the assembly changes. Closing either window ends what it draws.
- **Order with phase 11.** These are plain menu items and windows, as the app has them now; when phase 11's command registry and tiles land, they move into it under `measure.*` ids (`measure.tool`, `measure.mass`, `measure.clearance`).
- **Testing.** `core::measure` unit tests (picks and stale answers, lines, mass and clearance answers, what a selection weighs), and window tests: two faces measured by clicks (8 mm exact, nothing selected, Esc leaves the tool), and Mass properties and Clearance listing a frame of three blocks (blocks, since a curved body's certified mass and clearance take tens of seconds in a debug build, #195), Show framing the pair.

## Views and navigation in the app (decided in ryucad phase 10)

The design is document 198 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; these are its phases 3 and 4, plan 10.B.

- **The cube.** `core::cube` (geometry) and `ui::cube` (egui's painter) draw a navigation cube in the viewport's top-right corner, turned as the camera is but always orthographic: each face cut 3 × 3 at 0.6 of its half size, its middle the face's view, its sides and corners the edges' and corners' bevels, so its 54 tiles give the 26 views; faces seen squarely enough are labelled Front, Back, Left, Right, Top, Bottom (front is at -y, as `StandardView` has it). Four arrows turn the view a quarter turn about the screen's axes about the target (the eye moves that way), two roll it about the line of sight. A click on a tile turns to its view framing the model; picking, hover and the context menu never reach the model under the widget (`View::on_cube`). A Views button under it opens Perspective, Orthographic (keeping how big the model looks at the target), Isometric, Zoom to fit and the named views. `display.nav_cube` hides it.
- **Turns.** The cube, the standard views, the selector, the preset keys, named views and Normal to turn the camera over 250 ms (`core::nav::Turn`, `between`): its orientation by normalized quaternion interpolation (square roots only), eased, its target, distance, field of view and height straight, a change of projection kind at once; a drag, scroll or pinch stops it where it is. Each step is shared, so followers and the session see the camera move; it ends exactly on the view.
- **Triad and origin.** The bottom-left corner shows x, y and z in red, green and blue as the camera turns them, farthest first (`display.triad`). `display.origin` draws the shown model's origin axes and planes in the scene as `render --origin` does, a tenth of the model's diagonal, as depth-tested bands like sketch curves (`core::sketch::origin` over `bands`, which sketch curves now share).
- **Normal to.** The window asks the daemon (`camera.set` with `normal_to`, the geometry being the daemon's) and frames the answer's direction for its own aspect; it is enabled when this window has one face selected.
- **The view selector.** Space over the viewport (never while a text field has focus) opens the view selector at the pointer, as SolidWorks's: a cube to click, the six faces, isometric, dimetric and trimetric, Normal to, the shown part's named views (`core::views::NamedViews`, read with `view.list` when the shown part changes or its revision moves), and Save view…, which names the window's camera (a part ID, not a standard view's name) and saves it with `view.save`. Space again, Esc, a choice or a click elsewhere closes it.
- **Keymap presets.** `core::keymap` gives each `ui.navigation` style a table of default keys for the view and measure commands, each command with a registry ID (`view.front`, `view.front_top_right`, `view.normal_to`, `view.fit`, `view.selector`, `measure.tool`) and a title: `ryucad` FreeCAD's number keys (0 iso, 1 front, 2 top, 3 right, 4 back, 5 bottom, 6 left) and N normal to; `solidworks` Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6 front, back, left, right, top, bottom, Ctrl+7 isometric, Ctrl+8 normal to (SolidWorks's own order; the design listed Ctrl+7 as normal to); `onshape` Shift+1 to Shift+7 and N; all three F fit, Space the selector and M measure. A key is matched by what it types or, failing that, where it is (Shift+1 types `!`). The window runs them itself and the View menu shows them, until phase 11's registry reads the tables as named default bindings. Nothing binds a key outside these commands.
- **Menus.** View gains Dimetric, Trimetric, Normal to, Named views, View selector, Navigation cube, Axis triad and Origin; Preferences gains the three display toggles.
- **Testing.** `core::cube` (tiles, the 26 views from the middle of the cube, iso's three faces, arrows and their turns), `core::nav` (turns from front to every view, the shorter way round, a hand stopping them), `core::keymap` (each preset's keys once, none the window's own, Shift and a digit found by its place), `core::views` and `core::sketch` (the origin's bands) unit tests, and window tests: the cube's tiles and roll arrow turning the view without picking, Space opening the selector, a named view restored exactly and one saved into Git, and the preset keys of two styles.

## Appearance in the app (decided in ryucad phase 10)

The design is document 198 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its phase 5, plan 10.C.

- **Looks.** The window reads `appearance.get` for its focus (else the project's model) when it loads, when its focus moves, on `model.changed` and when the activity log names `materials.json`, the latest reading winning (`core::appearance::read`): each drawn body's look, by body key, a body drawn plain having none. `Model::looks` holds them, with what the Appearance window drags laid over.
- **Drawing.** The scene uploads a body once per hash, level and look (`Look::id`; 0 plain), so occurrences drawn alike share an upload and a colour change uploads vertices again but fetches no mesh; the `ryucad.mesh.1` format doesn't change (the review service decodes it). Opaque faces draw as before, in their colours. Translucent faces, and edges that bound only translucent faces (as opaque as the most opaque, found as the faces at all of an edge's samples), draw after everything else by weighted blended order-independent transparency (McGuire and Bavoil): depth-tested against the scene without writing depth, into an `Rgba16Float` accumulation and an `R8Unorm` revealage target multisampled as the scene is and resolved, then composited over the scene. Selections, highlights and hover paint over a translucent face opaque. A frame with nothing translucent runs no extra pass, so the other snapshots don't move. The navigation cube and triad are egui's, over the scene's image, so the scene's snapshot doesn't hold them.
- **The Appearance window.** Tools > Appearance opens it on the selection's first entity (a part's face, then its solid, then the part; an occurrence's solid, then the occurrences above it, chosen in a list), else the one part shown (an assembly alone colours nothing). A colour picker and an opacity slider show their change on the model as they are dragged (`Workspace::preview_appearance`) and are written once when let go, a hex field when Enter is pressed; Use material colour and Clear write at once; each is one `appearance.set` or `appearance.clear`, undoable from the Activity tab or Ctrl+Z. Opacity is written to a thousandth. A refusal stays in the window. Phase 11's registry and tiles take it over as `appearance.edit`.
- **Testing.** `core::appearance` unit tests (answers to looks, a preview's cover, targets, typed colours); the scene test drawing a part red, then translucent, then plain again; a window test colouring a selected face by its hex, undoing it with Ctrl+Z and writing a dragged opacity; the snapshot `appearance-lavapipe.png` (a blue part, a red top, a translucent boss).

- **Fonts and icons.** `ui::fonts` embeds Inter (text, egui's fonts behind it), JetBrains Mono (monospace) and Phosphor Regular and Fill (families `icons`, `icons-fill`); release archives carry their licences in `licenses/` (a test fails on a licence file `release.yml` doesn't copy). Up, Down, Enter, Esc.

- **Tiles.** `ui::shell` is an `egui_tiles` 0.18 tree of panes by tile id (`core::layout::TILES`: viewport, tree, properties, parameters, part, conflicts, changes, activity, history, releases, bom, shortcuts), each pane in tabs; `ui::tiles` draws each, `ui/panels.rs` split one module per tile (`panels` keeps the People strip, the compare bar, the status line and shared helpers). ## Sections and exploded views in the app (decided in ryucad phase 10)

The design is document 198 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; these are its phases 6 and 7, plan 10.D, built on 11.B's registry and tiles.

- **Following the session.** `core::people::Person` reads a participant's `sections` and `explode`; the window cuts with its own participant's planes (`Workspace::sections`, `core::section::Sections`) and shows its explode (`Workspace::explodes`, `core::explode::Explodes`, the shown assembly's `explode.list` read when it changes). An agent's `ryucad section` and `ryucad explode show` reach the window as they reach `hidden`.
- **Drawing cuts.** `ui::scene::Scene::cut` takes the planes each frame (`scene::Cut`, with the camera caps are lit for). Every shader discards fragments past a plane; while planes cut, the scene draws with a second set of pipelines on a `Depth24PlusStencil8` target (made the first time it cuts): faces both ways round, a back face nearest the eye a cap lit as the entry plane and hatched (`ryucad::render::HATCH`, `HATCH_SHADE`) that writes stencil 1, a front face 0; paint, edges, sketches, ghosts and translucent faces are drawn only where the stencil is 0, so nothing shows through a cap. A frame with no planes draws with the pipelines it always had, so no other snapshot moves. Caps take the colour of the faces they look into.
- **Picking.** `core::pick::Picker::with_sections`: a face, edge, vertex or ghost hit on the cut side is passed over, and where the nearest kept surface is a back face (a cap) nothing is picked and what is behind it is hidden.
- **Explodes drawn.** `core::model::Model::explode` holds each moved occurrence's `ryucad::explode::Offset`, applied after its pose in `Model::pose`, so drawing, picking and framing see it; offsets are worked out in the window from `explode.list`'s resolved steps (`ryucad::explode::offsets`), so the slider moves the model at once. Dashed trail lines (`ryucad::render::TRAIL`) run from each moved occurrence's middle (`Model::middles`) through each step, painted over the viewport like the measure tool's lines.
- **Handles.** `core::gizmo`: an `Arrow` dragged along its axis (`slide`, the pointer's motion along the arrow on screen) and a `Ring` turned about its axis (`turn`, the pointer's turn about its middle on screen, signed by which way the axis faces), each grabbed within `gizmo::REACH` pixels. The active plane shows an arrow along its normal and rings about its two in-plane axes (`ryucad::section::Plane::axes`), a tenth of the model's diagonal (`View::handle_length`), when `Sections::handles` is on; a drag moves the plane at once and writes `section.set` once, when let go. A new explode step shows three axis arrows at the selected occurrences' middle; let go, the step is written after the shown explode's steps (or as the first of a new one), its length to a thousandth of the default unit.
- **Tiles and commands.** `section` (Section) and `explode` (Explode) are on-demand tiles on the right (`core::layout::TILES`, `home`). The Section tile adds planes (XY, YZ, ZX, the selected face; three at most), lists each with its offset in the default unit and its normal's azimuth and elevation (`DragValue`s: dragged, moved at once and written when let go; typed, written at once), Flip and Remove, the handles' checkbox and Remove all. The Explode tile lists the assembly's explodes (one that doesn't read says so), shows one, plays it with a slider (`explode fraction`; dragged, drawn at once and written when let go), Collapse, its steps each with Remove (the last step's removal deletes the explode), warnings, and Add step from selection with a new explode's name. Registry commands (View): `section.edit` (Section view: opens the tile with its handles), `section.clear`, `explode.edit` (Exploded view, needs an assembly shown), `explode.collapse`; icons Phosphor's `knife` and `arrows-out-cardinal`. No preset gives them keys.
- **Testing.** `core::gizmo`, `core::section`, `core::explode`, `core::model` (an explode moves where occurrences are drawn) and `core::pick` (cuts and caps) unit tests; `tests/workspace.rs`'s `the_window_cuts_and_explodes_as_its_participant_does` against a real daemon; `tests/scene.rs`'s `a_section_cuts_and_caps` (a hatched cap, nothing cut drawn, no edge through a cap); window tests dragging a plane's arrow (written once) and choosing and playing an explode from its tile; snapshots `section-lavapipe.png` and `explode-lavapipe.png` (the scene's texture, so no cube, triad or trails, as 10.B's and 10.C's).

## The Equations tile (decided in ryucad phase 10)

The design is document 198 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its phase 9, plan 10.E, built on 11.B's registry and tiles.

- **The table.** `core::equations::Equations` reads `equations.list` (every part, no `--all`) when the tile first shows, again on `model.changed` and `project.changed` while nothing is being written (keeping the filter and what was typed), and after a reconnect. Rows show where each lives, its name (a parameter's, a field's pointer, a dimension's constraint), its expression to edit, its value in the default unit (or `fails`, the reason on hover) and on hover what it reads. A filter shows everything, the project's parameters, a part's rows or a feature's.
- **Writes.** Enter in a row, or its Apply, writes that row alone where it lives (`Equations::request`: `param.set`, `feature.edit` with `expressions`, or `sketch.edit`), against the revision the table was read at; a conflict reads it again and lists what changed under what was typed, as the forms do.
