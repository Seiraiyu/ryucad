## Tessellation (decided in phase 3)

The design is document 19 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`.

- **API.** `mesh::tessellate(&Body, &MeshParams) -> Result<BodyMesh, Error>`. `MeshParams` has an absolute `chord` (at least `10 × LINEAR_RESOLUTION`), an `angle` in `(0, π/2]`, an optional `max_edge`, and a `max_triangles` budget (50M by default). Bad parameters are `InvalidMeshParams`; a runaway request is `MeshTooLarge`, refused from an estimate before faces are meshed, and again if the real count passes the budget.
- **Guarantee.** Every face is within `chord` of its triangles both ways (two-sided Hausdorff), plus the local edge tolerance near tolerant edges. The bounds are certified, never sampled: `geom::{CurveBound, SurfaceBound}` bound second derivatives and turning rates over a parameter range (closed forms for analytic types, interval arithmetic on Bézier pieces for NURBS, differencing the whole piece before cutting it down), and a triangle passes when `M R² / 2 <= chord` (Waldron's bound, `R` the smallest enclosing circle in the face's scaled parameter plane). The normal turns at most `angle` across a triangle, except that the angle test is waived for pieces smaller than `chord` (features under the tolerance, and poles or apexes where the normal has no limit).
- **Structure.** Edges are sampled once (bisection until the curve and every adjacent face are satisfied, with an exact sample at every seam crossing; a sample already on the seam is that crossing) and shared, so the mesh is watertight by index. Each face is a constrained Delaunay triangulation on exact `orient2d`/`incircle` in its parameter plane scaled by powers of two (so scaling is exact; each direction's scale is the surface's speed at the middle of the box of the face's vertices, and of points along its edges where that box is flat, as for a face bounded by one closed edge), cut at seams (the domain start of a periodic direction) into one period, with collapsed rows (poles, apexes) as sides whose points weld to one position. Refinement is longest-edge bisection; a face never splits a boundary segment itself but asks its edge for a sample, and is meshed again (up to twelve rounds; after three, an edge still asked about is halved all along, so the asking stops). An edge's first segments are checked at their middle, so an arc of half a turn or more round a periodic face can't be mistaken for the short way round. A vertex whose neighbours meet one welded position in two runs (a "pinch" at a pole or across a seam) is repaired by flips or splits before assembly.
- **Stretched planes** (release 0.7.2, chosen anew in ryukei 15.B). A face whose triangles may run longer one way is meshed in a plane shortened that way by a power of two (#288); see Anisotropic planes below for how the plane is chosen and capped.
- **Output.** `BodyMesh`: welded `positions` (body vertices, then edge samples, then face points), per-face `corners` (position, unwrapped `(u, v)`, outward normal), triangles counterclockwise from outside, `FaceMesh` ranges by `FaceId`, `EdgePolyline`s by `EdgeId`, and `faults`. A face that can't meet the guarantees (`EdgeOffSurface`, `BadDomain`, `RefinementStalled`, `BoundaryNotResolved`) is left out and reported; `check_mesh` verifies indices, winding and that every welded edge has exactly two opposite uses.
- **Parallelism and determinism.** `ryukei` depends on `rayon`: edges, then faces, run on the current pool; each is a pure function and results are gathered in ID order, so the mesh is the same bits on any number of threads. Golden hashes pin meshes across platforms.
- **Doubly periodic faces.** A face with loops that leave one direction unwound (a torus band between two circles, a pipe's elbow) meshes that direction as open, wrapped from just below the face by its loops' orientation, when the loops leave a tenth of a turn clear. Otherwise (a face whose loops are all holes, a band spiralling round a torus, one within a tenth of a turn of that) both directions stay periodic, with seams in gaps between the loops' sampled extents (`uv::gap`, which sees spans running past the period's end); a ring that fits between one direction's seams but not the other's is cut at the seam it crosses, as on a cylinder. A band spiralling round a torus whose loops cover every height that way between them (one loop more than a turn wide, leaving no gap) takes its seam the other way, in that direction's gap, and is cut at the first; opening it would start its period inside the loop. Loops winding both ways, or fitting neither way, are `BadDomain`.
- **Slivers.** Booleans make faces meeting in wedges thinner than the chord. Where two faces draw one chord between boundary points (both inside, or one as a loop segment), both are meshed again with it split (`refine::chords`), however short it is (its midpoint is free, so neither half is a chord again), so the welded mesh stays manifold. Where two loop segments cross (edges of a sliver closer than their samples' sagitta), the face asks both edges for samples there. An edge alone in a loop with one other gets its middle when it would have no inner samples. A seam crossing is only accepted where the curve really lies on the seam (a long segment's side test can flip half a period away instead).
- **Crossings too short to part** (issue #370). Where two loop segments cross while each is already shorter in model space than refinement's floor (`waive / 16`) or twice its edge's tolerance, the face asks for no samples and is `BadDomain` at once: halving can't part them. A sewn sphere face whose tolerant edge ends a few micrometres from the pole, swinging round it, crossed itself there and doubled its edge's samples for twelve rounds in each of shell facing's five meshes, so sewing race body 1503 never returned; it was then refused `Uncertain` in about two seconds. `Cdt::illegal` also flips only a strictly convex quadrilateral (a guard: Lawson's flips over a constrained Delaunay triangulation never meet another).
- **Tolerant edges into a pole** (issue #497). On a face's second try (`FaceGeom::reseamed`, now also taken where a tolerant edge has a vertex on a collapsed row), the run of a tolerant edge's samples next to that vertex that lie within the edge's or the vertex's tolerance of the row goes, in the parameters alone, onto the straight line from the pole to the first sample outside the tube, evenly spaced in their order (`domain::into_pole`): in the tube model they are at the pole, but several samples can't weld to its one position (each is a point of the neighbouring face's mesh too), so each edge runs straight into the pole along its own meridian. The samples keep their points, so only the triangulation's structure changes. Race body 1503 sewn (two edges spiralling 2 to 7 µm round a sphere's pole) now meshes at every chord and sews back (`tests/sewing_hangs.rs`). Only faces whose first try failed take it, so no golden hash moved.
- **Grazed seams** (issue #449). A face on a surface periodic one way whose loops wind round nothing but come within a thousandth of the period of its seam (a file's fillet whose edge lies 15 nm past it, or that starts 1e-13 rad short of it), and whose domain can't be built cut there, is meshed again once with its seam in the widest gap its loops leave (`FaceGeom::reseamed`), its edges sampled again for it. Every face that meshes at its seam meshes as before, so no golden hash moved.
- **Limits.** Closed directions are periodic ones only (a clamped NURBS face whose ends meet is `BadDomain`). A ring through a pole never winds round its free direction (the jump along the pole is free, and with half a turn across it the nearest period is a tie), and a jump that crosses a seam is cut there; a ring through two poles (a sphere's wedge) takes the stretch between them by the face's orientation. A feature thinner than the chord (a torus tube) can stall refinement. Meshes are not FEA-quality, and ryukei makes about 1.5 to 18 times OCCT's triangles at the same chord.

## Anisotropic planes (decided in ryukei phase 15)

The design is document 296 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 15.B.

- **The plane.** Each face is meshed in its speeds' plane with one direction shortened by a power of two up to 1,024 (`uv::stretched`). The plane is the one an estimate from the face's ten samples says needs the fewest triangles. At each sample it takes the radius the chord allows (`M R² / 2`) and the radius the angle allows (from the normal's turning rates), the lesser of the two. A larger stretch, or the other direction, is taken only where it saves an eighth.
  - The samples' choice is capped by the whole-box bound's choice by the chord alone, the same direction.
  - Faces with poles, apexes or an unbounded turn bound keep their speeds.
  - One-way-curved faces (cylinders, cones, extrusions) stretch along their straight direction.
  - Two caps hold, the smaller winning:
    - the face's extent that way in the speeds' plane over the chord, so no direction is shortened below the chord;
    - `uv::SPANS` (4) times that extent over `h = sqrt(2 chord / κ_more)`, the size the chord allows a triangle across the more curved direction at its largest sampled curvature (ryukei 11.B, #367: a bent hole's wall straight through a 1 mm sheet, shortened 2¹⁸ times, never finished meshing at `measure::mass`'s chord; `tests/mesh_regressions.rs`' `a_refolded_bent_wall_meshes`).

    A 2 m cylinder 3 m tall stretches 128 times at a millimetre.
  - Waldron's bound holds in any scaling, so the guarantee is unchanged.
- **Bounds from jets.** Splines, extrusions and revolutions take a piece's `|S_kk|` and turning rates from one `Sheet::jet_box` (`bound::jet_bound`), not their `derivative_bound`: a revolution's enclosed its curve afresh, allocating, on every call.
- **Small pieces.** A piece whose diameter plus `|S_u| Δu + |S_v| Δv` is within the chord passes without the second-derivative bound (`bound::small_enough`). This is for parameters running fast along the surface, where `|S''|` is huge and mostly tangential.
- **Splits.** A triangle failing only the angle test is split along the edge its normal turns most along (`refine::edge_to_split`), so a stretched plane can't send refinement the wrong way to its floor (#255).
- **Tolerant corners** (#465). Where a tolerant edge's pcurve ends apart from its neighbour's at their vertex, the corner triangle between them keeps its size however finely the edge is sampled, so a face asked for samples there until the rounds ran out (`BoundaryNotResolved`; five bodies of the owner's files at mass's 1e-7 chord, a spline edge of tolerance 1.75e-6). A triangle failing its tests whose model-space diameter is within the tube of one of its corners (the tolerance, past `TOUCH_TOLERANCE`, of a curved edge's sample, or of a vertex a curved edge of the face meets, and the waiver's length) is tested again against the chord plus that tolerance with the angle waived up to it (`refine::in_tube`): the guarantee's chord plus the local tolerance, and mass's strips along curved edges, two slacks wide, hold such triangles. Exact bodies never pass `TOUCH_TOLERANCE`, so their meshes didn't move.
- **Tolerant vertices' parameters** (#483). A loop takes each vertex's `(u, v)` from its leaving edge's pcurve. At a tolerant vertex that pcurve may start microns off it while the arriving edge's ends on it, whose last samples then run past the corner and cross the leaving edge's (an owner's 0.15 mm cylinder patch, a line 3.9 µm off its vertex: `BadDomain` at mass's chord). A face whose loops make no region is gathered once more with each vertex past `TOUCH_TOLERANCE` at the arriving pcurve's end where that lies nearer it on the surface (`domain::gather_at_corners`); every face that built its region builds it as before, so no golden hash moved.
- **Slivers** (#337). Where an edge's rounded midpoint is already a vertex (the corner opposite it, on a sliver lying along the edge), refinement splits the sliver's other inner edges, the longer first, before calling the face stalled, at most `refine::SLIVER_SPLITS` (64) times a face. Only a triangle that would stall its face takes that path, so every face that meshed meshes as it did. `nist_ctc_05`'s spline face 68 needs 9 at mass's chord; without the cap, a face in healing's shell facing (`heal_props`' fault seed 393) took 232,741 in 70 s without ending.
- **What wasn't built.**
  - Design 296's normal-component bound saved 0.03% of the race's triangles for 27% more time: a bound over one triangle's patch must still pay for `P S''` along its edges.
  - Per-region metrics with metric flips weren't needed for the bar.
  - Mass's own phase (adaptive bound meshes) was no faster on these meshes, and its area bounds were looser.
- **Measured.** The race's 1,598 bodies through STEP, at 1 mm and 15°, one body after another on one thread, on cox-worker-2 (AMD Ryzen 7 9700X, shared):
  - main: 244.7 s and 37.3M triangles (planning);
  - 15.B: 53.9 s and 21.0M triangles, no faults;
  - OCCT: 11.3M triangles.

  - The bar (`ryukei-bench`'s `formats` test, devcontainer, all 8 cores, three runs on the same box with the host carrying other guests' load): ryukei 9.29, 8.62 and 8.62 s against OCCT's 13.21, 22.28 and 13.04 s, at 21,002,124 triangles against 11,278,534 (1.86 times). The test asserts design 296's bar: faster than OCCT, at no more than `TRIANGLES_BAR` (2) times its triangles.
  - The mesh benches' new cases (`ryukei-bench`'s `mesh`: a thin ring, a long rod, a block's long edge filleted), one thread at a chord of 1e-4: 32.6 ms against main's 172.6 ms, 1.53 against 31.0 ms, and 0.32 against 15.5 ms.
- **Work units.** The mesher still charges a unit per triangle tested, so its units fall with its triangles: the `work` golden section moved once.
  - Healing the pinned and committed corpora, every outcome is as on main, the largest heal 2,100,967 units (main 2,122,190, `nist_ctc_02_asme1_rc`). `heal.budget`'s 4× headroom rule already misses there by 1.2% on main (#368, 15.A's calibration).
  - The fuzz targets' largest cases (cox-worker-4, an AMD Opteron 6380, loaded): blend 213,842 and offset 4,022,398 over 900 s from empty corpora; sweep 6,228,652 over its 717 seeds' replay at fuzz.yml's 6 GiB (695 printed units before a four-hour limit, none failing, the slowest seed 362 s). All are under `FUZZ_BUDGET / 8`.
- **Testing.** Unit tests in `mesh::bound` (jet bounds against samples, the small-piece test against sampled deviation), `mesh::uv` (stretches and both caps), `mesh::refine` (the turn split); `tests/mesh_props.rs`' `race_bodies_mesh_within_the_chord` (103 race bodies sampled within the chord, release only), `tests/fuzz_regressions.rs` (#255's spline fillet at every chord and angle), `tests/mesh_regressions.rs` (#367), ryustep's `tests/mass.rs` (#307, #308, #337), `ryukei-bench`'s anisotropic mesh benches and `formats` race bar (OCCT, devcontainer).
- **Limits.** Cones, spheres and tori are still most of the race's triangles. A tolerant edge passing through a pole within its tolerance, its parameters swinging round it, is `BadDomain` (see Crossings too short to part). Blend seed 267 now meshes but healing still refuses it (#371).

## Spline bounds by Taylor cells (decided in the 0.8.x burn-down)

Issues #307 and #308 (and the shell meshes of #239 and #305).

- **Why.** Every triangle tested on a NURBS face enclosed the patch's jets over its box by cutting the homogeneous net and its five derivative nets down to it by interval de Casteljau: about 23,000 interval lerps a box for a degree 6 × 10 rational patch. `measure::mass`'s fine mesh tests millions of boxes; on OCCT's `linkrods.step` that was nearly all of its 1,572.6 s mass on cox-worker-4 (AMD Opteron 6380, one thread, load 6 to 12).
- **The rule** (`geom::nurbs::bound::taylor`, `NurbsSurface::jet_box_small`, used by `mesh::bound`'s `jet_bound` and `small_enough` only). Each patch's cells are built on first use: each way a level of its own (the whole patch, halves or quarters, on two grids half a cell apart), so a box takes the smallest cell holding it along each direction; the mesher's stretched planes make boxes long and thin in the parameters, which square cells sent back to the cut. A cell holds its nets of orders 0 to 4 cut to it, centred on it. A box is enclosed about an f64 de Casteljau evaluation at its middle of the orders 0 to 3, by the lesser of a mean value remainder (`|N_u|` over the cell times the box's half-width) and a second-order Taylor remainder (the gradient evaluated there and half the Hessian's bound over the cell), plus the coefficients' radius and a derived rounding bound (`7u (p + q) M`); the quotient rule is `patch_jets`'. A cell whose nets aren't finite leaves its boxes to the cut. Cells live in one process-wide table by surface id, patch and cell, sharded 16 ways, each shard two generations of at most 8 MiB (a full young generation becomes the old one, and a cell found in the old one moves back), so at most 256 MiB; a cell is a function of its key, so neither eviction nor which thread built it changes a bit. Kept on the patches (#545 as merged), KiCad's Smolhaj model held 4.29 GB at its peak against main's 1.17 GB. Everything is plain f64 and interval arithmetic through `ryu_math` rules, no FMA, so the same bits on every platform; one thread and many build the same cells.
- **Interval products** take the two bound products the bounds' signs pick, not all four, the same bits (a unit test over every sign, zeros, subnormals and infinities).
- **What moved.** Certified as the cut is, not the same bits: meshes of spline faces moved, bodies didn't (every body dump of the moved sections compared with main's), and `check_mesh` holds. Golden sections `meshes` (196,586 → 197,110 triangles), `general_booleans` (33,584 → 34,812), `lofts` (5,260 → 5,390), `spline_blends` (4,102 → 4,114), `mirror` (38,076 → 38,078), `nurbs_race` (2,384 → 2,428), and ryucad's `lofts` (its render).
- **Measured** on cox-worker-4 (AMD Opteron 6380, 8 threads, one rayon thread a case, `ryustep`'s `examples/check-split` with `CHECK_SPLIT_MASS=1`, main and this side by side, load 4 to 8), mass main → this: `linkrods.step` 1,572.6 → 130.3 s (its normal-quality mesh 276.6 → 44.0 s, peak RSS 771 MB), `nist_ftc_07` 25.9 → 12.7 s, `nist_ftc_10` 12.8 → 9.3 s, KiCad's `SMA_Molex_73251-2200` 123.5 → 46.8 s, OCCT's `nurbs_cylinder` 11.7 → 9.2 s; volumes within their bounds of main's.
- **Tried and left.** Power-basis coefficients evaluated by Horner (`O(p q)` a net) gave the same meshes but was slower over all (converting each cell's nets costs more than its boxes save at the display mesh's density), and converting the interval coefficients themselves widened the floor by `2^(p + q)`.
- **Testing.** `taylor::tests` (120 seeded rational surfaces, degrees 1 to 7, scales from a micrometre to a metre, boxes from a point to nine tenths of the domain: the enclosures hold f64 evaluations at sampled parameters; cells chosen per direction; point boxes tight), `interval::tests::products_by_sign_match_all_four`, and the golden sections above.

## Taylor cells at speed (decided in release 8p3)

Issue #308: KiCad's `Smolhaj_Scale_0.1.step` body 5 (116 faces, 100 of them splines, 98 of those polynomial) weighed in 68 s on cox-worker-2's Ryzen at #548, its fine mesh 7.46M triangles from 15.6M triangle tests.

- **Where it went.** Sampled on cox-worker-1 (Intel Xeon E5-2650, one thread): interval rounding (`interval::up` and `down` through `next_up`) a quarter of the fine mesh, the Taylor remainder's interval expressions most of that, then `jet_box_small`'s patch scan (#551's, fixed there), clearing `KnotVector::basis`'s 26 by 26 table on every call, and the quotient rule.
- **Bit for bit.** The remainder's radius takes upper ends alone (`interval::add_up`, `mul_up`: every term is non-negative, so each interval step's upper end is the round-to-nearest result of the upper ends stepped up), the floats the interval expressions gave; `interval::up` and `down` judge the bits rather than going through `next_up`'s general cases; f64 de Casteljau and `KnotVector::basis` take tables of 8 for degrees below 8; `NurbsSurface::point` and `eval_first` take `eval`'s sums for the orders they give (`basis_to` stops at the order asked for), and mass's quadrature and the mesher's new points take them; the angle test evaluates a stretched piece's middle only when its whole turn fails (waiving only takes terms out of the sum); each thread keeps the four Taylor cells it asked for last (a cell is a function of its key).
- **Polynomial patches.** A patch whose weights are all exactly 1 takes its jets as the first three components' enclosures: no weight's radii and no interval quotient rule, which only widened them by dividing by an enclosure of 1 and subtracting the point times enclosures of 0. Never wider (a test compares both on the same cells), so the mesh is never finer; body 5's fine mesh gained 2 triangles (7,456,246 → 7,456,248) and its volume and bound kept their bits. No ryukei golden section moved.
- **Tried and left.** The remainder in plain f64 with one inflation by `1 + 32u` took a tenth off the fine mesh, but it moves every spline mesh's bits and can be a few ulps looser than the stepped expressions. A memo of verdicts across a face's rounds: only 5% of tests repeat a triangle of an earlier round.
- **Measured.** Body 5's mass on cox-worker-2 (AMD Ryzen 7 9700X, one thread, three runs, 2026-11-07): main 63.4, 63.9 and 63.2 s, this 39.2, 39.3 and 39.3 s, the same bits; still over step-hunt's 30 s, so #308's remainder went to v0.9.0. Side by side on cox-worker-1 (Intel Xeon E5-2650, one rayon thread each, load 2.5 to 5.1): main (a553ba6a, with #551's patch spans) 334.3 s, this 189.2 s (1.77 times). The other #307/#308 cases (`nist_ftc_07`, `nist_ftc_10`, OCCT's `nurbs_cylinder` and `linkrods.step`) are none slower, their volumes and bounds the same bits.
- **Testing.** `taylor::tests::radii_by_upper_ends_match_the_interval_expressions` (200,000 draws from zeros, values under `TINY`, near overflow, infinities and NaN), `polynomial_jets_are_no_wider_than_the_quotients`, `enclosures_hold_sampled_values` over polynomial surfaces too, `interval::tests::steps_on_the_bits_follow_the_rule`, `basis::tests::small_tables_give_the_same_bits`, `surface::tests::lower_orders_are_evals_bits`, and every golden section held.
