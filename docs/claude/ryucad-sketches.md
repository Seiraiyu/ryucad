## Sketch features and sweeps (decided in ryucad phase 2)

The design is document 36 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; these are its phases 3 and 4, plan 2.B.

- **Features.** `doc::files::Feature` is an enum: `Primitive` (phase 0's types, `PrimitiveType`), `Sketch`, `Extrude`, `Revolve`. Sketches and sweeps are feature format 2. Quantities may be angles (`units::Field`): a bare number in an angle field is degrees.
- **Sketch files.** A sketch lies on an origin plane (`plane`: `xy`, `yz`, `zx`, `x` along the first axis) or a `placement`. Entities (`point` with `m`, `line`, `arc` counterclockwise, `circle` with `r_m`, `construction` on curves) and constraints (`a`, `b`, `about`, and a `value` expression for dimensions) are objects keyed by ID; IDs are unique across both, generated as `p3`, `l2`, `a1`, `ci4`, `c7`, `d4`. Positions are solver state, f64 meters with `-0` written as `0` (`sketch::clean`), so a write and a read give the same bits. Validation goes through `ryusketch::Sketch::validate`, its faults mapped to the file's IDs and pointers.
- **Solving.** `ryucad::sketch::solve` maps IDs to `u32`s in ID order. Stored positions that already hold come back with the same bits and rebuild uses them as stored; otherwise the sketch is stale, rebuilt from positions solved in memory with a `sketch_stale` warning (in the rebuild result and the CLI envelope); one that can't hold is `sketch_unsolved`. A sketch's rebuild output (`rebuild::Output::Sketch`) has no body: the rebuild report gives its state, dof and staleness instead of a hash, and it has no mesh.
- **Editing.** `sketch.edit` applies a batch of ops (`add`, `remove`, `set`, `move`) with one solve and writes the solved positions, or refuses the whole batch (`sketch_overconstrained`, `sketch_unsolved`, `has_dependents` for a curve's point or what a sweep names); what was moved is held where it was put for a first solve, so it stays there if the rest can follow. Removing an entity removes the constraints that name it. `sketch.solve` reports without writing, for the sketch or a proposed batch; `sketch.get` gives positions in both frames and per-entity and per-constraint states. Writes keep sketches current (see References and recompute below). Staleness from outside is never written behind anyone's back: `doctor` reports `sketch_stale` and its repair writes the solved file.
- **References.** `<part>/<sketch>/<entity>` names a sketch entity in selections, highlights and framing (`entity::Target`). An extrude or revolve names a sketch listed before it: `feature.add`/`edit` refuse a missing sketch (`not_found`), one that isn't a sketch (`reference_wrong_kind`), one after it (`reference_forward`), and entities it doesn't have; `feature.delete` of a swept sketch is `has_dependents`, `feature.move` that would put a sweep before its sketch `reference_forward`.
- **Sweeps.** Profiles come from the solve report's closed loops (`ryucad::profile`), all or those holding a chosen entity; segment keys are SipHash of `ryucad.sketch\0<part>\0<sketch>\0<entity>`, loop keys the same over the loop's anchor: the first of the points where its curves join in natural order (`p2` before `p10`; a lone circle's anchor is the circle), and generated IDs are one past the largest, never a gap, so dimensions, extents, which way lines run and new segments or fillets rename nothing but what sat where a segment joins; removing the anchor point renames both caps and every cap edge. Extrude is along the sketch's normal (`blind`, `symmetric`, `reverse`); revolve about a line of the sketch (`angle` up to 360 deg or `full`, `symmetric`). A sweep whose sketch failed is `dependency_failed`. Kernel faults come back in the sketch's terms: `profile_invalid` with `entities`, `invalid_extent`. `inspect` gives every sweep entity an `origin` (its sketch entity, cap and loop, or the entities either side of it), derived by recomputing the sweep's names from ryucad's keys (`Name::from_u128`, the one ryukei API this phase added); bounds and display meshes of sweeps come from the body's faces.
- **Testing.** `tests/sweeps.rs` (names across edits, `check_intersections`, thread count), the `sweeps` section of `tests/golden/rebuild.txt`, CLI scenarios both ways, and the `feature` fuzz target, which solves sketch files and checks the result is current.

## References and recompute (decided in ryucad phase 2)

The design is document 36 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its phase 5, plan 2.C.

- **References.** A feature refers to a face, edge or vertex of an earlier feature of its part by stable name only: `{"feature", "name"}` in files (`doc::reference::Reference`), `<part>/<feature>/<name>` in requests and output. `reference::resolve` finds exactly the entity with that name or fails: `not_found`, `reference_forward`, `dependency_failed`, `reference_wrong_kind` (a sketch, or the wrong kind of entity), `reference_unresolved` (the name is gone; with `role` and `reference`, and `was: {kind, from}` when the session has seen the name: the session remembers every name of every body it built; and `candidates`, what the name became through the solid's journals (`rebuild::successors`), written as the reference was, left out when there are none, #156). Never a nearest match, never a signature.
- **Uses.** A sketch on a planar face (`face`, with an optional vertex `origin` and straight edge `x`; proto and CLI `face`, `face_origin`, `face_x`, `""` for the default): the face's outward normal, the part origin and `x` (or `y` when `x` is along the normal) projected onto it. Projected entities: any point, line, arc or circle may carry `projected`; a vertex fixes a point, a straight edge a line's points, a circle parallel to the sketch a circle's centre and radius or an arc's points (counterclockwise in the sketch); the solver holds them fixed (`sketch::Context`), and `add` makes a projected curve's points when they are left out. Edges neither straight nor a parallel circle, or that project to a point, are `projection_unsupported`. Extrude `up_to` a planar face parallel to the sketch or a vertex, plus an `offset` of either sign (`invalid_extent` if not parallel or no further than the sketch). Revolve about a straight edge in the sketch's plane (`invalid_extent` off it). Writes check that references resolve to the right kind now; geometry is rebuild's.
- **Graph.** A feature's inputs (`graph::inputs`) are its swept sketch and its references' features, always earlier in the list; its depth orders the rebuild, each depth in parallel. `feature.delete` of anything another feature reads is `has_dependents`, `feature.move` that would put a reader before its input `reference_forward`; `feature.list` gives each feature's `uses`.
- **Recompute.** Each result is keyed by a BLAKE3 hash of its inputs: ryucad's and ryukei's versions, the feature as written (canonical, units explicit), the values of the parameters it reads, and each input's place and result hash. The session keeps each part's results by feature (`rebuild::Cache`) and reuses every unchanged one; bodies are shared behind an `Arc`. `tests/recompute.rs` checks incremental equals full over random edits at 1 and 8 threads.
- **Keeping sketches current.** `param.set`, `feature.edit` and `sketch.edit` note which sketches are current before they write and write back, solved, those the write left stale, in the same logged change (`sketches` in the response); one that now fails is reported and left. `doctor` judges sketches that read other features by rebuilding their part, and its repair's files include everything they read.
- **Testing.** Unit tests per reference use, `tests/recompute.rs`, the `references` section of `tests/golden/rebuild.txt` (bodies pass `check_intersections` too), a CLI scenario both ways, and the `feature` fuzz target, which builds each file after a box and a sketch.

## Sketches in the app and renders (decided in ryucad phase 2)

The design is document 36 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its phase 6, plan 2.D.

- **Drawing.** `ryucad::sketch_view` says how a solved sketch is drawn, for `sketch.get`, `render` and the app alike: each curve a polyline in model space (lines exactly, arcs and circles in pieces of at most the display quality's angle, never under 8 to a turn), each point a dot, each entity in its solve state (`well`, `under`, `over`, `failing`), and each dimension a label: its value as written, with `= <value> <default unit>` (degrees for angles) when it isn't a literal, placed beside what it measures (4% of the sketch's size off a line's middle, past a circle at 45°, along an angle's bisector). `sketch.get` takes a `quality` (the user's by default) and adds `polyline` to curves and `label` to dimensions.
- **Renders.** `render` draws sketches (`sketches`, default true; CLI `--no-sketches`) in fixed state colours (`render::SKETCH_*`), construction curves dashed on screen (6 on, 4 off), points as dots, highlighted sketch entities in their highlights' colours, and with `labels` each dimension's label. Golden: the `sketch_drawing` section.
- **App.** The window fetches each sketch's `sketch.get` when its part's revision moves (and when the display quality or default unit does) and draws it in the scene, depth-tested and pulled towards the eye as edges are: curves as bands two points wide, points as squares, so a sketch on a face shows and one behind a solid hides. Sketch points pick like vertices and curves like edges (`Target::Sketch`); a sketch chosen in the tree is framed and painted. `display.sketches` (default true) turns sketches, their labels and their picking off. Every sketch is drawn, whatever reads it.
- **Labels and forms.** Dimension labels are drawn where no face hides their anchor; clicking one opens its value in the property panel, saved with `sketch.edit` (`set`) against the revision `sketch.get` read; a conflict reloads and says what changed, as the feature form does.
- **Parameters and the tree.** The parameter panel shows the chosen feature's part (else the first part): each parameter as written, its value and what reads it, editable, with a row to add one, saved with `param.set` against `param.list`'s revision. Rebuild reports give each feature's `uses`, and the tree shows them under each feature, with a sketch's state and freedom and a failure's code.
- **Testing.** `tests/sketches.rs` and `tests/params.rs` against real daemons; window tests pick a sketch line, edit a dimension and a parameter, and read the tree; the viewport snapshot has a sketch on the box. lavapipe crashes when window tests draw in parallel (in the devcontainer, and on the CI runners from 2026-10-22), so each window test holds one lock (`one_at_a_time`) for its whole run.

## Sweeps along paths (decided in ryucad phase 14)

The design is document 323 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 14.A (phases 1 to 3).

- **Files.** `sweep` (feature format 8, `doc::sweep::{Sweep, Section, Source, Law}`): `profile` `{sketch, loops?}` or `{face: {feature, name}, loops?}` (one `Section` type, which 14.B's lofts take too), `path` any curve (a path or helix `{feature}`, an edge `{feature, name}`, a sketch's line, arc or circle `{feature, entity}`), `law` `minimal` or `binormal` with `direction` (any axis, or a plane's normal), always written; `twist` an angle and `scale` a positive number, written only when given; `operation` and `targets` as an extrude's. A profile's `loops` names an entity on each loop (a sketch's), or an edge or vertex of the face by name. `Feature::swept()` became `Feature::profiles()`, every sketch a feature sweeps or drills at.
- **Face profiles.** `profile::of_face`: a planar face's loops, every one or those `loops` names, in the frame a sketch on the face takes (`sketch::face_frame`: outward normal, the part origin projected, the part's `x`), each segment its edge's exact curve in that plane (`pcurve::exact` on a plane in the frame), keyed `ryucad.section\0<part>\0edge\0<name>`, each loop over its least vertex (`ryucad.section\0<part>\0vertex\0<name>`). A face that isn't planar is `reference_wrong_kind`; loops that make no profile `profile_invalid` naming edges.
- **Paths.** `path::of_curve` gives what a sweep follows: a path or helix as built, else the one curve keyed as a path's segment (`path::edge_key`, the sketch's entity key), so a sweep along one curve names what it makes as one along a path of that curve; ryukei's path checks run on it.
- **The default law.** A new sweep that names none gets `minimal`, or along a path that is exactly one helix (a helix feature, or a path of it alone) `binormal` with `direction` the helix itself (`{feature}`: a helix is its axis where a field takes an axis), written into the file, so editing the path later never changes how the sweep is carried.
- **Building** (`ryucad::along`). `sweep::along_with` under the feature's `OpKey` and the request's cancel (`work::work`), no budget; combine as an extrude's (the swept body kept by its own inputs' hash; `tool_hash` reads its profiles and geometry). Round a closed path a twist that isn't whole turns or a scale other than 1 is `invalid_param` at `/twist` or `/scale`, when written and when rebuilt. Origins are derived from the keys as the kernel names: a side `{entity | edge, path_segment}`, a lateral `{entities, path_segment}`, a section and a joint's vertex `{…, path_segments}`, caps `{cap, loop}`, cap edges and end vertices as an extrude's, each with its source (`sketch` or `face`) and `feature`. Rebuild reports and `inspect --feature` give `sweep`: the profile's loops (`anchor` and entities), the path's report, law, direction, `twist_rad`, `scale`, worked out again from the rebuild's results (the disk cache's bytes are unchanged).
- **Refusals.** `sweep_failed` (new code) with `reason` (`profile_along_path`, `path_too_curved`, `frame_undefined`, `not_closing`, `bad_scale`, `self_intersecting`, `uncertain`), `kernel`, and `path_segment` (a leg's source) or `faces` (`<part>/<feature>/<hex>` with their origins); a profile's faults are `profile_invalid`, a path's its own codes.
- **Protocol and CLI.** `feature.add`/`edit` take `sketch` or `face`, `loops`, `path`, `law`, `direction`, `twist`, `scale` (`""` for none), `operation`, `targets`; CLI `--sketch`, `--face`, `--loop`, `--path`, `--law`, `--direction`, `--twist`, `--scale`, `--operation`, `--target` (the design's `--join`/`--cut` are extrudes' `--operation`). `law minimal` drops a direction; a direction alone makes the law binormal. Writes check references' kinds, the profile's sketch and loops, the path's checks and a closed path's twist and scale (`along::check`).
- **Testing.** `doc::sweep` unit tests, `profile` (a box's top face, narrowed, a cylinder's side refused), `path::tests::one_curve_is_keyed_as_a_path_of_it_would_be`, `merge`, `diff`, `renumber` unit tests; `tests/sweeps_along.rs` (a cylinder, a circle round a bend of a line and a tangent arc against Pappus, that bend cut from a block checked clean to its exact volume, a twisted square and a frustum with names unchanged across edits, a spring up a helix against Pappus about its axis, a face swept and joined, refusals; the spring, the twisted square and the threads release only, a step in `full.yml`'s release suites); `tests/cli.rs`'s `sweeps_from_the_cli` both ways; `tests/recompute.rs` with a sweep among its edits (its path's end moved, its law switched: a twisted or scaled sweep took about 25 s a rebuild in a debug build, which a test rebuilding from nothing after every edit can't afford); the twisted and scaled cases are `sweeps_along`'s and the golden's; the `sweeps_along` golden section; the `feature` fuzz target's sweep seeds and the `merge` target's `0xf5` mode.
- **Limits.** Two kernel limits, pinned in `sweeps_along` so a fix shows: a profile with flat sides meeting at a vertex (a triangular or trapezoidal thread) swept up a helix is refused `sweep_failed` with `reason: uncertain`, the self-intersection check unable to part its sides along their lateral; and a round thread, which sweeps, can't be cut from a cylindrical shank (`boolean_failed`, `uncertain` on the shank's side), whatever its turns, pitch, depth or law (cox-worker-1, 2026-11-02). So the design's thread cut from a shank waits on ryukei (#376).

## Lofts (decided in ryucad phase 14)

The design is document 323 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 14.B (phases 4 and 5).

- **Files.** `loft` (feature format 8, `doc::sweep::{Loft, Tangent, TangentDirection}`): `sections`, two or more (three or more when `closed`), each a sweep's `Section` (`{sketch, loops?}` or `{face: {feature, name}, loops?}`); `closed` written only when true; `start_tangent` and `end_tangent` `{direction, weight?}`, `direction` `normal` or any axis or plane reference, `weight` a positive number (1 when left out), never with `closed`; `operation` and `targets` as an extrude's. Too few sections and a weight that isn't positive are `invalid_param`, a tangent on a closed loft `invalid_document`, each at its pointer. `Feature::profiles()` gives every section's sketch once, in order; a face section is a reference with role `sections.<i>`.
- **Correspondence** (`ryucad::loft::sections`). Each `loops` entry names one loop and where it starts: a sketch point or a face vertex starts it there; a curve or an edge at its first point along the loop as the loft runs it; a lone circle at its only point. Entries naming one loop twice, or a point two loops hold, are `invalid_param`. The `i`th loop of every section takes the first section's `i`th loop's key. A section with no `loops` takes its one closed loop (several are `invalid_param` at `/sections/<i>/loops`); the first starts at its anchor (`profile::anchor`, a face's least vertex name), each later one at its vertex nearest where the section before starts once both loops' centroids are brought together, ties in natural order. Sections listing different numbers of loops are `loft_failed` `loop_mismatch`; matched loops with different numbers of curves `segment_count_mismatch`, naming the section, the loop's curves and both counts, with the hint to split curves in the sketch. ryucad never splits curves.
- **Facing.** A centroid is the mean of eight points along each curve, evenly by parameter (a circle's is its centre); a section's is its loops' mean. A section whose normal points against the line to the next section's centroid (the last: from the one before; round a closed loft, the last to the first) is turned over: its frame a half turn about its `x`, its curves mirrored across it, so nothing moves and every loop runs counterclockwise about the loft from its start. An exact zero (a section within `ANGULAR_RESOLUTION` of edge-on, since centroids are sums and an exact zero rounds away) is `bad_section`. `profile::Keys` gained `points`, where each sketch point and face vertex at a loop's joins lies in the plane.
- **Tangents.** A tangent's direction is the end section's normal facing along the loft, or the reference's direction made to face along it; its length is the weight times the straight distance between the first and last sections' centroids.
- **Building.** `sweep::loft_with` under the feature's `OpKey` and the request's cancel, no budget; combine as an extrude's (the lofted body kept by its own inputs' hash). Origins are derived from the keys as the kernel names: a side `{loop, sections: [{sketch, entity} | {face, edge}]}`, a lateral and its vertices `{loop, sections: [{…, entities}]}`, caps `{cap, loop}`, cap edges with their side's fields and `cap`. Rebuild reports and `inspect --feature` give `loft`: each section (its source, `turned`, and its loops in order with `anchor`, `start`, curves from the start and `segments`), `closed`, and each tangent's `direction`, `weight` and `length_m`, worked out again from the rebuild's results.
- **Refusals.** `loft_failed` (new code) with `reason` (`too_few_sections`, `loop_mismatch`, `segment_count_mismatch`, `bad_section`, `bad_tangent`, `bad_weights`, `self_intersecting`, `uncertain`), `kernel`, `section` (`{index, sketch | face}`) with its `pointer`, and `entities` (the first section's curve whose skin's weights fall to zero) or `faces` (`<part>/<feature>/<hex>` with their origins). Writes check every section's sketch and loop entities, face sections' planar faces, tangents' references, and the correspondence against the current rebuild (`loft::check`; a section whose sketch fails now is left to rebuild).
- **Protocol and CLI.** `feature.add`/`edit` take `sections` (each text, a sketch's ID, `<part>/<sketch>` or a face's reference, or `{section, loops}`; given, they replace them all), `closed`, `start_tangent`, `end_tangent` (`""` drops one), `start_weight`, `end_weight` (`""` back to 1), `operation`, `targets`; CLI `--section` repeated, each `--loop` after the section it narrows (read from the command line as written; before every section is `usage`), `--closed`, `--start-tangent`, `--start-weight`, `--end-tangent`, `--end-weight`, `--operation`, `--target`.
- **Fixed on the way.** `sketch.edit` of a sweep's profile sketch reached an `unreachable!` (14.A wired sweeps into `sweeps_of` but not the check of what they name); a sweep's and a loft's loop entries now stay while they are named (`has_dependents`).
- **Testing.** `doc::sweep`'s loft round trips and refusals; `tests/lofts.rs` (a frustum and a prism, a quarter turn pinned by `loops` against Simpson's two thirds, a section facing back turned over, a box's face lofted to a sketch and joined, three circles on datum planes, end tangents, names across a section's resize and move, refusals, a ring of three refused; a ring of six against Pappus, release only); `tests/sweeps_along.rs`'s `a_profiles_sketch_edits_under_its_sweep`; `tests/cli.rs`'s `lofts_from_the_cli` both ways; `tests/recompute.rs` with a loft among its edits; the `lofts` golden section; the `feature` fuzz target's loft seeds and the `merge` target's `0xf3` mode.
- **Limits.** The kernel refuses sections whose normals turn a right angle or more from the one before (`BadSection`), so a closed ring needs five sections or more round a full turn: the design's ring of three is refused `bad_section` with the hint to add sections between, and the tests' ring has six.
