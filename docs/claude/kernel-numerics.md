## Numerics (decided in phase 0)

The design is document 12 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`.

- **Units and tolerances.** Model space is SI meters. `ryukei::tol` fixes `LINEAR_RESOLUTION` = 1e-8 m, `ANGULAR_RESOLUTION` = 1e-11 rad and a size box of ±500 m for every body. Per-entity `Tolerance` values are never below the resolution. Unit conversion and larger models are ryucad's job.
- **Precision policy.** Decisions that change topology or classification (side of a plane, inside a circle or sphere, ordering) use exact or certified predicates (`ryukei::predicates`, `ryukei::interval`), never a raw float sign. Geometric values (coordinates, parameters, distances, constructed points) are f64 compared through `tol`, never with `==`. Predicates take their f64 inputs as exact. An uncertain interval sign must be escalated or resolved by an explicit tolerance rule, never guessed.
- **Determinism.** Kernel code gets transcendental functions only from `ryukei::math`; `clippy.toml` bans the `f64` versions and `mul_add`. `tests/golden/determinism.txt` holds the bit-exact hashes that every CI platform and CPU level must reproduce. When you change results on purpose, regenerate it and review the diff.

## No subnormal bounds (decided in the 0.8.x burn-down, #301)

- **Why.** Interval arithmetic widened every round-to-nearest bound by one ulp, so an exact zero became `[-5e-324, 5e-324]` and every product of it another subnormal. CPUs that handle subnormals in microcode (Intel before Ice Lake, AMD before Zen) take about a hundred cycles an operation: on cox-worker-1 (Intel Xeon E5-2650) NIST FTC 10's self-intersection check took 61.5 s, and 31 s with subnormals flushed (DAZ/FTZ); a Ryzen 7 9700X doesn't pay it, which is most of the "15 to 160 times" the check ran on older CPUs.
- **The rule.** `interval::down` and `up` judge the round-to-nearest bound before stepping it: within `TINY` (`2^-511`, about `1.5e-154`) of zero, a lower bound becomes `0` when it is positive and `-TINY` otherwise, an upper bound `0` when negative and `TINY` otherwise. So no bound is subnormal and `TINY²` is still normal. Intervals only grow, so they still hold the true value, and every bound outside that range has the bits it had; a sign is lost only for values within `TINY` of zero, far below any tolerance. Exact zeros are not kept exact (`0 × x` is still widened): that would make results an ulp tighter everywhere and moved 18 golden sections.
- **What moved.** On release/0.8, the `offsets` golden section (main's `bent` too, a section 0.8 doesn't have): certified derivative bounds of quantities that are exactly zero (a flat direction's curvature) went from `5e-162` to `2e-77` (`√TINY`). Nothing else in ryukei's goldens.

## Tiny coordinates rescaled (decided in the 0.8.x burn-down, #308)

- **Why.** The predicates' filter and expansions need every coordinate zero or at least `2^-100`; anything smaller went to `bigint::Fixed`, integers scaled by `2^1074` (about a thousand bits a coordinate, four thousand a product). A mesh point a rounding residue off a seam or a pole (`1e-40` beside millimetres) sent every `incircle` and `orient2d` touching it there: mass's fine mesh of KiCad's SMA connector spent nearly all its 175 s in big-integer `incircle` (cox-worker-1, Xeon E5-2650).
- **The rule.** `orient2d`, `orient3d`, `incircle` and `insphere` are homogeneous, so scaling every coordinate by one power of two keeps their sign. Where some coordinate is below `2^-100`, `bigint::rescale` finds the power that brings the smallest non-zero one to `[2^-100, 2^-99)`, if the largest then stays below `2^100`, and the predicate runs on the scaled coordinates (`bigint::scale2`, exact: a power of two times a float only grows here, and stays finite). Coordinates spanning more than `2^199` still go to big integers. The signs are exact either way, so nothing moved; the SMA connector's mass took 7.7 s. `volume_sign` and `winding_sign` are unchanged.
