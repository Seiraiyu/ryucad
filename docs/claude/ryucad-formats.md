## Mesh formats (decided in ryucad phase 8)

The design is document 193 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.A (phases 1 to 3).

- **The export method.** `crate::export` builds a format-free `Scene` (design 120's products: a part's solids as shown, an assembly's instances and copies at their solved poses, ids, names, descriptions, revisions, usages and each child's instance step) and lowers it to each format; STEP's lowering is the old code, its bytes unchanged. `export` takes `format` (`step`, `stl`, `3mf`; else `out`'s extension, `.step`/`.stp`, `.stl`, `.3mf`, in any case; else STEP) and `out`, an absolute path the daemon writes itself (`ryu_doc::source::write_atomic_with`: streamed into a temporary file beside it, synced, renamed), its folder required (`not_found`; a relative path is `invalid_param`): the answer gives `out`, `bytes` and the BLAKE3 of what was written, and without `out` the bytes follow the answer as before. The CLI always sends `--out` absolute and no longer writes bytes itself. Options another format takes are `invalid_param` naming them: STEP takes `schema` and `mm`/`m`; mesh formats `mm` (default), `cm` or `m`, `chord` (default `0.01 mm`), `angle` (default `15 deg`, in `(0, 90 deg]`) and `max_edge`, literals only (`export::MeshOptions`: a bare length in the project's unit, a bare angle in degrees; a chord under `10 × LINEAR_RESOLUTION` or a longest edge under the chord refused), and STL `ascii`. The time stamp is reported only where a format writes it (STEP's header, 3MF's `CreationDate`).
- **Export meshes.** `mesh::Cache::export_all` meshes each distinct body (by hash) once at the export's `MeshParams`, in parallel, kept in memory under the parameters' bits beside the display meshes and on disk in the `meshes` kind by body hash and parameters; display meshes are now held as `Arc<BodyMesh>`. A body past ryukei's triangle budget is `export_too_large` (part, revision, solid, `triangles` from ryukei's estimate, `max_triangles`); faces ryukei can't mesh are `export_mesh_faults`, every one listed (part, revision, solid, the face's reference `<part>/<solid>/<name>`, ryukei's fault in snake case, and the edge's reference for an edge fault). Answers give each product's own `triangles` and the `mesh` options as quantities.
- **`ryuformats`.** A pure-Rust crate beside `ryustep`, over `ryukei` (meshes, `Rigid`) and `miniz_oxide`; never `ryucad`, `ryustep` or C++, and ryukei never depends on it. Its `Scene` is products of solids' meshes in their own frames placing each other (`Child`: a product index, a `Rigid` in meters, the instance's ID), the root first; `Scene::placed` walks every occurrence depth first, a product's solids before its children. `Unit` multiplies once in f64; numbers are the shortest text that reads back to the same bits, never exponents, `-0` as `0` (`text`). Writers stream into an `io::Write`.
- **STL.** One file of every solid of every occurrence placed in the root's frame (`stl::write`): binary by default (an 80-byte header `ryucad <version> <part> <unit>` padded with spaces, the count, little-endian `f32` triangles, attribute 0), or ASCII with a `solid <path>` block per placed solid (`<root part>/<instance>/…/<solid>`); positions placed and scaled in f64 and rounded to `f32` once, each facet's normal its triangle's from the f64 positions (zero where it has no area). More than `u32::MAX` triangles in binary is refused. An empty scene is a valid empty file.
- **Zip and 3MF.** `zip::write` writes entries in the order given, each deflated by `miniz_oxide` at level 6, DOS-epoch times, no extra fields or comments, no ZIP64 (an entry or offset past 4 GiB is refused): the same entries, the same bytes. `threemf::write` is the 3MF core specification 1.3 with no extensions: `[Content_Types].xml`, `_rels/.rels`, `3D/3dmodel.model`. Objects are numbered by a walk writing what an object uses first: a product of one solid and nothing placed is that solid's mesh object, any other an object of components (its solids' mesh objects, named by their solids, without transforms, then each child's object with its pose as a 3MF `transform`, the rotation's columns as rows and the translation in the file's unit); objects carry the product's `name` and `partnumber` (its identifier), a product placed many times is one object, a product showing nothing has no object, and the build holds the root's object if it has one. Metadata: `Title` (the root product's name), `Application` (`ryucad <version>`), `CreationDate` (the time stamp), and `ryucad:chord`, `ryucad:angle` and `ryucad:max_edge` as written, in the namespace `https://seiraiyu.com/ryucad/3mf/2026`.
- **References.** The devcontainer builds lib3mf 2.5.0 from its pinned source release (SHA-256 checked; Debian packages 1.8, older than core 1.3) into `/usr/local`, headers in `include/lib3mf`. `ryukei-bench`'s `formats` feature links it through a cxx shim (`lib3mf::read`: strict mode, warnings, unit, metadata, objects, and the build's triangles and volume with every component placed); the `occt` feature gains `occt::read_stl` (`RWStl::ReadFile`, the reader under `StlAPI_Reader`, which welds coincident vertices; `TKDESTL`), its triangles judged closed by index (`OcctMesh::closed`): OCCT 7.8's `StlAPI_Reader` gives a face carrying the triangulation, not shells to close. The `formats` test (features `occt,formats`, in `full.yml`'s devcontainer job) reads every seeded race body that meshes as binary and ASCII STL and as 3MF, the bodies placed three times each on a grid in one STL and one 3MF, and ryucad's exports of a drilled plate, a pin, a part of two solids and an assembly, each to the volume ryukei's meshes enclose (within `f32` rounding), the exports within the chord times the area of the exact volume (on cox-worker-1: 36 of 36 seeded bodies, and the grid's 775,416 triangles, in about 100 s in release).
- **Testing.** `ryuformats` unit tests per module (scene walk, units, text, STL bytes both ways, the zip against the `zip` crate, 3MF objects, components, transforms, escaping), its `tests/stl.rs` and `tests/threemf.rs` (independent test readers, `zip` and `roxmltree` dev-dependencies only: watertight read-back and the placed volume in every unit); ryucad's `export.rs` tests (out written by the daemon, formats by extension, options checked, mesh options, STL of a part and an assembly with a pattern, 3MF objects and metadata, too large, faults named), `mesh.rs`'s export meshes, and `tests/cli.rs`'s `exports_from_the_cli` and `mesh_exports_from_the_cli`, both ways. Golden hashes for every format and the `export` fuzz target are plan 8.D's.
- **Limits.** A writer holds part of what it writes in memory (STL a solid's placed positions, 3MF the whole model, deflated once); positions closer than an `f32` step in the file's unit become one vertex, so a sliver thinner than that is a degenerate triangle there.

## USD (decided in ryucad phase 8)

The design is document 193 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.B (phases 4 and 5).

- **The export.** `export` takes `format` `usda` (or `out`'s `.usda`), in `mm` (default), `cm` or `m`, with `brep` and `mesh` (both by default; CLI `--no-brep`, `--no-mesh`; neither is `invalid_param` at `mesh`), a mesh's `chord`, `angle` and `max_edge` (refused without meshes) and `pipe_tolerance` (default `0.0001 mm`, STEP's 1e-7 m; refused without B-rep, and under resolution). The answer adds `brep`, `mesh` (where meshes are written), `pipe_tolerance` (where B-rep is) and the time stamp, which goes in `customLayerData`. A face the BrepArray can't describe is `export_unrepresentable` with `part`, `rev`, `solid`, `face`, `entity` and `reason` (`seams`, `pcurve`, `domain`, `approximation`), before anything is written. usdc and usdz are the same layer in other encodings (see usdc and usdz below).
- **The layer model.** `ryuformats::usd::layer`: a layer's metadata, prims (`def` or `class`, a type, metadata: prepended API schemas, `customData`, `instanceable`, `kind`, internal references) and attributes (`uniform`, `custom`, an interpolation; the value types the stage writes, finite only), names checked when made. `usd::usda` writes it canonically: layer metadata, prims and attributes in the order made, four spaces a level, a blank line before each child prim, numbers as the shortest text that reads back (never exponents, `-0` as `0`), strings quoted with `\`, `"` and control characters escaped. The same layer, the same text.
- **The stage.** `usd::stage::layer` from a `Model` (products with each solid's body and, where meshes are written, its mesh): Z up, `metersPerUnit` the unit's, `defaultPrim` the root, `customLayerData`'s `ryucad` dictionary the export's settings and, with B-rep, `omni_solid` (the usd-brep commit whose schema is written). A part alone is a root `Xform` (kind `component`) of an `Xform` per solid; an assembly defines each leaf product (a part at a revision, `<part>` or `<part>_<label>`) once as a `class Xform` (kind `component`) under `class "Prototypes"`, and places it with an `Xform` per occurrence, `instanceable` and referencing it, its pose a `matrix4d xformOp:transform` (rows the axes' images, the translation last, in the stage's unit); subassemblies are nested `Xform`s (kind `subassembly`), never instances; the root is kind `assembly`. A product's own solids come before its occurrences. Prim names are IDs made identifiers (`-` and `:` to `_`, `_` before a digit, `_2`, `_3` on collisions in order); `customData` keeps what each prim stands for: a product's part, revision, identifier and name, an occurrence's instance ID, a solid's `<part>/<solid>`. Each distinct body's BrepArray is made once, in parallel.
- **Meshes.** A solid's `mesh` is a `Mesh` of ryukei's export mesh (8.A's, by body hash): welded points in `f32` in the stage's unit, triangles, each corner's normal (`faceVarying`), `rightHanded`, single-sided, no subdivision, `extent`, and `primvars:ryucad:face` (`uniform`): each triangle's index into the BrepArray's faces, which are numbered by name (`brep::face_order`). Without B-rep the mesh carries `ryucad:faceName` itself.
- **BrepArrays.** `usd::brep::brep_array` writes one brep per body from `heal::seamed_with_origins` (the shape STEP writes, with each entity's body origin): the infinite void region with each solid's outer shell, then each solid region (its outer shell and each cavity's, seen from the material) followed by each cavity's void region; two faceuses a face, `same` on the side the surface's natural normal points to (the void's for a face of sense `Forward`). Faces in name order, the outer loop first (the one enclosing most), loops counterclockwise about the surface's natural normal (a `Reversed` face's run the other way), holes clockwise, lone vertices as loops of no edgeuses (`loop:vertexIndex`, else 9999999 as usd-brep writes). Each edge's two edgeuses name each other as next round it; an edgeuse enters from the bottom exactly when it is `same`, as usd-brep's own OCCT reader writes. Edges and vertices in the shape's order. Planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori as their APIs (ryukei's parameters are the schema's); NURBS surfaces clamped, a periodic direction cut (`NurbsSurface::part`) at the start of the band its face's curves lie in; extrusions and revolutions their exact NURBS over the face's range; offsets (since plan 8.E) their NURBS forms within `pipe_tolerance` on the same parameters (`Offset::to_nurbs_within`), the face's tolerance growing by the bound (`approximation` where that fails). Lines (a unit direction, the range in the stage's unit), circles and ellipses (their range moved by whole turns to end in `(0, 2π]`, BA.630) and NURBS edges (a periodic one as the part the edge runs over).
- **UV curves.** Every edgeuse's curve is a NURBS on its edge's range as written (so SMLib, which maps one onto the other, reads it on the edge's parameter), running as its loop does: the shape's pcurve, else the body fin's (by its origin, on a surface the shape kept), else `pcurve::exact`, else `pcurve::fitted` (new and public: the fit `heal` uses, certified, its bound joining the tolerance). On an extrusion's or revolution's NURBS a seam keeps its constant through the `Reparam`; other curves are found again on the NURBS. Lines and splines stay on the edge's parameter exactly; a circle or ellipse in the plane is its rational form, exact in shape, its knots moved affinely onto the range. Each face's curves are moved by whole periods into one band of each periodic direction: from the seam across it, else from a cut in a gap the curves leave (the widest first) that leaves the loops enclosing a positive area, then moved by whole periods to start in the surface's first period (SMLib frees an array twice rebuilding a torus over a band from 2π); within a loop each curve follows on from the one before, but after a pole. Linear parameters (planes, cylinders' and cones' `v`) are in the stage's unit. `face:range` is the box of the face's curves, no longer than a period, held to a bounded domain; `face:trimType` is `rectangular` for one loop of four lines along the parameters round it. A loop whose curves can't meet head to tail within 1e-7 (one through a pole, where the loop jumps along the pole row) gives its edgeuses the schema's `(0, 0)` record: usd-brep's validator wants every authored loop closed within 1e-6 (BA.763), and SMLib trims from the edges.
- **Tolerance, extent, names.** `brep:intersectTol3d` is the largest tolerance of the body's edges and vertices, a face's approximation and a fitted pcurve, at least resolution, in the stage's unit; `brep:extent` the faces' boxes, every control point and vertex, grown by it; the prim's `extent` holds it in `f32`, rounded outward (BA.040 to BA.050). `ryucad:faceName`, `ryucad:edgeName` and `ryucad:vertexName` (`custom`) give each entity the body's name; a seam, or a vertex a seam added, has `""`, and a piece of an edge a seam split keeps the edge's.
- **Refusals.** `BrepError { face, reason }`: `seams` (`heal::seamed`'s `BadDomain`), `pcurve`, `domain`, and `approximation` for a pipe or sweep that won't approximate (naming the least-named face, as STEP's writer does).
- **The kernel.** `heal::seamed_with_origins` (`Origins`: each shape face's body face, each edge's body edge or none for a seam, each vertex's body vertex or none; `seamed`'s shape unchanged), `pcurve::fitted`, and `NurbsSurface::part(dir, range)`. `seamed`'s seam no longer runs along an edge of a winding loop (`crossings` reports a use with two neighbouring samples on the line, and `choose` passes that line by): a cone cut through its axis by a block's face took a seam on the ruling an edge already ran on, two edges of one face overlapping, which SMLib trimmed as more than the face (race body 16; `heal_props`' `seams_never_run_along_an_edge`). That moved ryukei's `heal` and ryustep's `write_m` and `write_mm` golden hashes; no other.
- **References.** The devcontainer installs CPython 3.12 with a pinned uv (trixie has 3.13; usd-brep's wheels are 3.12 only) and, in `/opt/usd`, usd-core 26.8 and usd-brep 1.0.1 with its validator (usd-validation-nvidia 1.23.0, which takes usd-core below 26.9; its omniSolid schema is SMLib's pin's, 1.0.0's, unchanged). `ryukei-bench`'s `usd` test (feature `usd`, no C++) runs `tests/usd/reference.py` (`RYUCAD_USD_PYTHON`, else `/opt/usd/bin/python`): OpenUSD opens and flattens each file and counts its BrepArrays, meshes, instances and prototypes; usd-brep's validator (the BrepArray rules, `brep_only`) passes it; SMLib imports it strictly, in a process of its own per file (a crash or the hang a corrupted heap leaves is that file's `import_error`), each brep a manifold solid of ryukei's measured volume within its bound and a millionth. Seeded race bodies each as a part, and ryucad's usda of a drilled plate, a pin, a ball and an assembly placing them (four instances of three prototypes). On cox-worker-4 (AMD Opteron 6380, 8 threads, 16 GB, Debian 12 in an LXC, Linux 6.17.2-1-pve), in the devcontainer, release: 34 of 37 seeded bodies written (three refused as `domain`), every one opened, valid and imported to its volume, in about 400 s.
- **Testing.** `ryuformats` unit tests per module (layer names, usda text, prim names, meshes, the stage's layout, transforms), its `tests/usd.rs` with `tests/common/brep.rs`, a check of a BrepArray written from the schema and usd-brep's validator rules (sizes, faceuse pairs, radial cycles, loops chained at vertices and head to tail in UV, every UV curve on its surface along its edge and on its edge's range, outer loops counterclockwise, ranges and frames, extents) over primitives in two units, holes, cavities, lumps, NURBS bodies and 16 seeds of race bodies, and `tests/common/usda.rs`, a reader of the usda subset written (a stage written, read and written again is the same text). ryukei unit tests for origins, fitted pcurves and surface parts; ryucad's `export.rs` tests (a part, an assembly's instances, options, a face named) and `tests/cli.rs`'s `usd_exports_from_the_cli` both ways. Golden hashes and the `export` fuzz target are plan 8.D's.
- **Limits.** A loop through a pole carries no UV curves; analytic faces' UV curves are written but SMLib discards them (it builds its own); a circle or ellipse in the parameter plane is on its edge's parameter only at its ends.

## usdc and usdz (decided in ryucad phase 8)

The design is document 193 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.C (phases 6 and 7).

- **The export.** `export` takes `format` `usdc` (or `out`'s `.usdc`, or `.usd`, which OpenUSD writes as crate bytes by default) and `usdz` (`.usdz`), with usda's options and refusals (`Format::is_usd`); the stage is the same layer, only its encoding differs. A usdz's root layer is `<part>.usdc`, named by the part rather than the file, so where it is written changes no byte.
- **usdc.** `ryuformats::usd::usdc` writes a `Layer` in Pixar's crate format 0.8.0 (OpenUSD's default for new files; `pxr/usd/sdf/crateFile.cpp`): the 88-byte bootstrap, values, the `TOKENS`, `STRINGS`, `FIELDS`, `FIELDSETS`, `PATHS` and `SPECS` sections compressed as 0.4.0 and later compress them, and the table of contents. Each spec (the pseudo-root, each prim, each attribute) is a path and a field set; booleans, integers, tokens, strings, specifiers and variabilities are inlined, everything else written once (equal values share their bytes) and referred to by offset. Integer arrays of 16 or more are compressed as OpenUSD compresses them; other arrays are written as they are (0.6.0's float compression is optional for readers), and no double is inlined as a float. Tokens (the first OpenUSD's `;-)`), strings, paths (ancestors first), fields and field sets are numbered in first use, and dictionaries are written in their keys' byte order, as OpenUSD keeps them, so `customLayerData` and `customData` read back sorted. `-0` is written `0`, as in usda; a number that isn't finite is `InvalidData`, as is a token or string holding NUL (the token table is NUL-separated, and a STEP product name can decode `\X2\0000\X0\` to U+0000). Values are shared by a hash of their bytes compared against what is written, so no second copy is kept.
- **Compression.** `usd::compress` is `TfFastCompression`'s framing of LZ4 blocks (`lz4_flex`, pure Rust with its safe encoder: a zero byte and one block up to `LZ4_MAX_INPUT_SIZE`, else a chunk count and each chunk's size) and `Sdf_IntegerCompression`'s coding (differences, the most common one written once, the larger on a tie, two-bit codes four to a byte from the low bits, then the other differences as `i8`, `i16` or `i32`). The same input, the same bytes.
- **usdz.** `ryuformats::usd::usdz` packs the usdc as one stored entry; `zip::write_stored` stores each entry's data on a 64-byte boundary from the archive's start, padded by an extra field with OpenUSD's ID `0x1986` (at least a header long, a whole alignment more when four bytes won't fit), version needed 1.0, DOS-epoch times. 3MF's deflated `zip::write` is unchanged in its bytes.
- **References.** `ryukei-bench`'s `usd` test writes every seeded body and ryucad's exports in all three encodings; `tests/usd/reference.py` gives each file the SHA-256 of its root layer as OpenUSD writes it as text (`Sdf.Layer.ExportToString`), so a usdc and a usdz must read to exactly the usda's layer, runs usd-brep's validator on each and OpenUSD's usdz package validators (`UsdzPackageValidator`, `RootPackageValidator`, `FileExtensionValidator`, `PackageEncapsulationValidator`) on each usdz, and imports only the usda into SMLib (the others are its layer). On cox-worker-2 (AMD Ryzen 7 9700X, 16 threads, 32 GB, Debian 13, Linux 6.17.2-1-pve), in the devcontainer, release: 34 of 37 seeded bodies written (8.B's three `domain` refusals), every usdc and usdz read by OpenUSD 26.8 to its usda's layer and valid, each usdz passing the package checks, ryucad's frame four instances of three prototypes in all three encodings, in 83.4 s. A usdc is about a third of its usda (race body 10: 88 MB against 281 MB).
- **Testing.** `usd::compress` unit tests (OpenUSD's own coding example, ties, sizes, wrapping, framing) and `zip`'s (padding, stored archives aligned and read by the `zip` crate); `tests/common/usdc.rs`, a reader of the usdc subset written (the bootstrap, sections, path tree and value reps, LZ4 and integer coding undone), and `tests/usd.rs`'s `usdc_reads_back_to_the_same_layer` (the same layer with sorted dictionaries, and the same bytes written again), `usdc_refuses_numbers_that_arent_finite`, `usdc_refuses_tokens_and_strings_holding_nul` and `usdz_is_the_usdc_stored_and_aligned`; ryucad's `export.rs` (`a_part_exports_as_usdc_and_usdz`, extensions and refusals) and `tests/cli.rs`'s `usd_exports_from_the_cli` both ways. Golden hashes, the `export` fuzz target and the app's dialog are plan 8.D's.
- **Limits.** A usdc is built in memory before it is written (values are referred to by offset, and the sections follow them); a usdz holds that buffer again while it is zipped. The framing counts at most 127 LZ4 chunks (about 250 GB of one section), OpenUSD's limit too; past it the writer panics, long after memory has run out.

## The app and hardening (decided in ryucad phase 8)

The design is document 193 in the Coxswain planning project `ryucad-ryukei`; this is its plan 8.D (phases 8 and 9).

- **The app.** File > Export… writes every format; see Export in the app in `ryucad-app.md`.
- **Goldens.** `ryucad::export::export_stamped(session, ask, version)` is `export` with `version` where STL's header, 3MF's `Application` and USD's layer data name ryucad's (STEP's header names ryustep's, which `exports` already unversions), so a golden pins every encoding's bytes whatever a release stamps. The `formats` section of `tests/golden/rebuild.txt` hashes binary and ASCII STL, 3MF, usda, usdc and usdz of an assembly with a subassembly and an instance pattern, the blended part, the swept part and a spline revolution imported from OCCT's file, at a chord of 0.05 mm and 20 degrees (CI runs the golden in debug builds), each project built and exported on one thread and on four. The blended part's rolled corner (a 0.6/0.4 mm torus) is refused as a BrepArray (`domain`, #247): the section pins the refusal and that part's stage with meshes alone, so fixing #247 moves the hash.
- **Fuzzing.** The `export` fuzz target makes scenes of one to three seeded bodies (`ryukei_testkit::blend_bytes::body`: primitives, sweeps, booleans' results), meshed at 5 mm, in parts of one or two solids placed on a grid of positions and quarter turns, directly or through a subassembly, in each unit, and writes them through every writer of `ryuformats`; it includes ryuformats' test readers by path (`tests/common`, with `zip`, `roxmltree` and `lz4_flex` as the fuzz crate's dependencies): STL's triangles alike both ways and enclosing the scene's volume, 3MF watertight by index and of that volume, the usda read and written again to the same text, the usdc read to the same layer as the usda's (dictionaries sorted, `common::usdc::sorted`), the usdz holding the usdc's bytes, every BrepArray passing the schema's checks; a body a BrepArray can't describe is a typed refusal. `fuzz.yml` runs it (6 GiB, as the boolean targets).
- **Fixes the race found.** `Spline::part` snaps an end of its range within its slack (a few ulps of the range) onto a knot: a circle's pcurve cut a rounding error past a knot kept a one-ulp sliver span, which collapsed to an end multiplicity of five when USD's lowering moved its knots onto the edge's range, and `brep_array` panicked (`an increasing range`). A UV curve the lowering can't remap is now a `pcurve` refusal, never a panic. No golden hash moved. Of the race's 1,598 bodies 1,429 write as BrepArrays; 162 are refused as `domain` and 7 as `pcurve` (#247).
- **The bars.** The race's parts are a metre or so across, so both races mesh at a chord of 1 mm and 15 degrees, a thousandth of a part as the default 0.01 mm is of a 10 mm one (at 0.01 mm the race would be about 5 × 10⁸ triangles): a departure from design 193's "default chord", agreed with the coxswain. `tests/step_bar.rs` exports the race's root, after its STEP export and from the same daemon, as usdc with meshes alone (`--no-brep`: one part refused as a BrepArray refuses the export, #247) and then 3MF (the same meshes, from the session's cache), 41,992,874 triangles, and prints every number before judging any bar. On cox-worker-4 (AMD Opteron 6380, 8 threads, 16 GB, shared with a fuzz lane at a load of 6 to 10), in the devcontainer, release, two runs: usdc in 593 and 609 s (43 to 52 times the STEP export's 11.5 to 14.2 s; 2.0 GB), 3MF in 398 and 414 s (28 to 36 times; 652 MB); a third run under a load of 56 took 1,498 and 587 s. The bars are `USDC_BAR` 80 and `THREE_MF_BAR` 55 times the STEP export (ten times looser without `RYUCAD_BARS`). On that CPU design 120's cold and warm bars failed in every run (cold 1.04 to 1.45 times the read, warm 5.6 to 15 s), loaded or not: they were recorded on a Ryzen 7 9700X. `ryukei-bench`'s `formats` test (`meshing_and_writing_the_race_as_stl_against_occt`) has both kernels read one STEP file of the race's bodies, mesh it at 1 mm and 15 degrees on all cores and write one binary STL: ryukei (`mesh::tessellate` per body, then `ryuformats::stl`) against OCCT (`BRepMesh_IncrementalMesh` in parallel, then `StlAPI_Writer`, through the shim's `mesh_stl`, timed in C++ around those two). Design 193 asked that ryukei be faster; on cox-worker-2 (Ryzen 7 9700X, loaded) ryukei took 245 s for 41,988,036 triangles and OCCT 30 s for 11,294,849, about 8 times its time at 3.7 times its triangles, ryukei's mesher certifying the chord both ways. Speeding it up is kernel work (#250, which names the slowest bodies); the test prints both kernels' times and triangles and ryukei's ten slowest bodies, and holds ryukei within `RACE_BAR` (10) times OCCT's time: a departure from the design, agreed with the coxswain.
- **Fuzzing, recorded.** An hour of the `export` target (`-max_len=512 -len_control=0`, 6 GiB, fork mode) on a shared 8-core worker at a load of 25 to 48: 934 executions, 238 inputs in the corpus, no crash, timeout or running out of memory. Run it with `cd fuzz && cargo +nightly fuzz run export -- -rss_limit_mb=6144 -max_len=512 -len_control=0`.
- **Limits.** Exporting the race's 42 million triangles is slow in every mesh format: on the Opteron 3MF writes them, from cached meshes, at about 100,000 triangles a second (a single deflate stream), and usdc's arrays are written uncompressed. The `formats` golden pins #247's refusal, so fixing it moves that hash.
